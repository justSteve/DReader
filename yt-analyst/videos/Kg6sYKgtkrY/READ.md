## The argument

He opens with the question he asks of every market: risk on or risk off, meaning is capital currently flowing into asset classes that carry risk, the Magnificent 7 among them, or into more defensive ones. He sets the fundamental stories aside; what the chart and above all the liquidity show is enough to make a plan: the level from which rejoining the long side makes sense, or the level that says wait for a pullback or look for a short. He is explicit that this is his "technical approach in the long-term sector". This is the swing-horizon application of PbD; the day-trading version is [the beginners' video](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md), and the only place the letters are actually explained is [the crisis video](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md), where he calls D a balance phase and P and b the aggressive trend phases up and down. Here he uses "b-structure" and "P-waves" without expansion, as everywhere else on the channel; reading the letters as shapes a profile draws is our gloss, not his.

The method is the same on each chart. First the daily candles with the PbD logic: where did large capital push price aggressively, where did it come to balance, and has a break out of that balance gained acceptance in the old balances or not. Between two balances he sees "just space", which "market profilers would partly call single print areas": a borrowed label for a large participant moving price through there without trading. Then the Market Profile, on a weekly basis, to check whether liquidity confirms what the candles suggest. The channel's full profile vocabulary lives in [the Market Profile primer](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md) and [its follow-up](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md); this video assumes it.

On the S&P he first has little to say: stable, moving up. Then the tip. Price sits in a range well above where the year ended, the major market players only come back the following week, and there is a relatively high probability that the market returns to around the year-end level. He says immediately that this is not a suggestion to take a short; he holds it conditionally: everything above the last range is oriented long for him, so a pullback into that area is where he waits patiently for a long setup, and only if no viable long setup appears does he look for a setup to go significantly lower. He points at a level tested once and twice and expects a third visit, and says that anyone who knows the channel's approach knows that at some point a level like that "just completely snaps". That a repeatedly defended level is a weakening one is a reading Carmine Rosato reaches from the tape in [his support-and-resistance episode](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md): a bounce made by aggressive buyers is a level waiting to break. The presenter reaches it from structure alone; neither man is watching the other's evidence.

The profile section is the most valuable minute of the video. A profile that had formed and looked like a correction left its POC "still open" in the area of 6,840. Over the last three weeks a balance formed and has now been broken to the upside, and the current week's liquidity sits above it, but the week is young. Then the discrepancy: the volume-weighted POC is up here, the time-based point of control from the Market Profile is down there. This is the POC-versus-VPOC distinction that [the primer](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md) draws and the "anomaly" that [its follow-up](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md) names, where the price the market traded most size at diverges from the price it spent most time at. This is the one place in the sweep where the anomaly is applied to a live S&P chart to produce a stance, and the stance is caution rather than a trade: short-term longs still make sense because the lower level produced a nice counter move, but not all-in. He closes the section by sending the reader back to the candles with 6,900 as the lower boundary to remember, and says it "could definitely go down again to that level".

His closing scenario is that liquidity is simply thin, because much of the move came around the turn of the year when the global players had left, and the new week will show whether the trends are real. It is the same premise as the S&P tip, and the honest frame for the whole video: dated levels, conditions attached, nothing executed. There is no entry, no stop, no target, no risk-reward and no hit rate here; the channel's numbers on those live in [the statistics video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) and [the 80 to 90% claim](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md). What this video adds is scorable: 6,840 and 6,900 on a named week of January 2026, the same treatment [the May outlook](../0CrkbfuIhkc/CARD.md) invites with its 6,850 line. The "World Champion" of the title remains unverified across the sweep.
