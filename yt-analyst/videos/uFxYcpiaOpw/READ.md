# Read: My Simplest Trading Strategy (80–90% Win Rate)

> **The channel's boldest number: an 80 to 90% hit rate on a once-a-day breakout out of balance, with the setup described in full and the risk not described at all.**

- **Channel:** Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge · **Uploaded:** 2026-07-22 · **Duration:** 9:33
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uFxYcpiaOpw

## In brief

He is arguing that the fast, high-probability trade does exist, but only in
the roughly 30% of the time when the market is out of balance, and that most
traders fail because they run the same setup straight through the other 70%.
So the method is a regime filter first and a trigger second. Weekly and daily
volume profiles say whether the market is balanced. Rising daily VPOCs say
which way it is leaning. An open outside the prior balance says it is leaving.
And a P-shaped profile on the one-minute chart, broken by a candle that closes
above it, is the proof that aggressive buyers are actually present.

Why it matters to us: this is the only place the channel puts a win rate on a
specific setup, and it never puts a risk-reward beside it. At 80% you break
even at a risk-reward of 0.25, so the claim is not implausible, only
untestable. The one number that could refute it is the one he leaves out.

## The argument

He opens with the hit rate and then spends the next minute narrowing it. The
model works about once a day. It works only in 30% of market phases. The
failure of "well over 90%" of traders is not a bad setup but a setup applied
in the wrong regime. That 70/30 split is the channel's standing figure: the
market-profile video has balance about 70% of the time, the volume-profile
video has a return to fair value in about 70% of phases. So the real thesis is
that a high hit rate is a property of the regime, not of the pattern. He names
PbD as the way to read the regime and never expands it; the crisis-PbD video
resolved it for the corpus as profile shapes, P for upward imbalance, b for
downward, D for balance.

The first worked chart is a place not to trade, which is good teaching. This
week's ES profile sits inside last week's, so the market is in equilibrium and
"the probability is initially relatively small that the market will make a
fast, abrupt movement". The daily cash-session profiles show flat VPOCs, so
the last few days were balance too. His conclusion is patience: imbalance is
above or below a balance, so wait for the market to leave.

The Nikkei is the positive case, with two conditions. Location: this week
opened above last week's balance. Reaction: the open dipped into the value
area, was rejected, and "was then aggressively traded upwards". At the weekly
scale that is break-and-retest, Carmine Rosato's entry and the pullback of
this presenter's own volume-profile video. Then the daily profiles: the VPOC
"is rising further and further up", a strong upward trend "in terms of the
distribution of the balance of market participants". That phrase is the
video's order-flow content. Trend is the migration of where volume is
accepted, not a sequence of higher highs. A single print at the open above the
prior day's value area then marks where aggressive buyers came in.

Proof comes from the one-minute chart: a P, which he defines as aggressive
buyers shifting their balance upward, and then the breakout, identified by its
closing price. On screen he types the framework in German, `IDEE: das der
Markt schnell wird` and `BEWEIS durch ein Break Out Setup`: "IDEA: that the
market becomes fast" and "PROOF through a breakout setup". This is the
idea-and-proof pair from his volume-profile video with a different idea inside
it. There the idea was a price level; here it is a hypothesis about speed, and
the breakout is the evidence that speed has arrived. This entry is on the
break, where the volume-profile video enters on the pullback; the channel
teaches both and never says when to use which. A closing price as trigger
matches the close-counting of the PbD videos, though he never contrasts a
close with a wick.

Then the argument stops, and what it stops short of is the point. There is no
stop distance, no target, no tick measurement and no risk-reward anywhere in
the nine and a half minutes. Break-even risk-reward is (1 − p)/p, so 80% needs
only 0.25 and 90% only 0.111. The claim is therefore not implausible;
high-hit-rate systems are normally small-reward systems. But without the
risk-reward it cannot be evaluated at all, and the omission runs in the one
direction that protects the headline.

It also sits unreconciled against his own framework. The volume-profile video
asks for 200 to 300 trades before figures mean anything and works an example
at a 50% hit rate giving an average risk-reward of "maybe a two". They may be
different setups, but the channel never says which regime a viewer is in, and
at once a day the sample he demands takes the better part of a year.

The channel's best counter-argument is its own. The VWAP video warns that a
trader may win 20 times adding counter-trend to VWAP and be liquidated on the
21st. That is negative skew stated plainly, a high hit rate hiding a fat left
tail. This setup trades with the trend, so the failure mode differs, but the
principle holds: the hit rate is the part of the statistic you can see.

Costs are never mentioned. Our own ES tick work puts a $17 round turn at 0.17
R on a 2-point stop, the kind of stop a one-minute entry implies. Against a
break-even of 0.25 that is most of the margin; by our arithmetic it lifts the
break-even to about 0.46 at 80% and 0.30 at 90%.

He does concede the hard part at the end: "waiting a very long time for that
exact moment". The promise at the top, that this breakout is taken "where very
few people believe a breakout will occur", is never returned to.
