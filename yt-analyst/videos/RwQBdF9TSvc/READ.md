## In brief

Stop-hunting, the guest says, is a myth, and he offers a test anyone with a
footprint chart can run: look under the lows and count the contracts. Sometimes
two or three, sometimes one, sometimes nothing. Whatever is happening down
there, it is not institutional size taking retail stops for fills. Nobody knows
where the stops are except your broker, and a large position is built over a
day or longer, sometimes weeks, through spreads and option hedges, inside a
range where prices are fair, not in a one-minute dip below a swing low. What
looks like a hunt is pain: the overweight side runs out of money and sells at
market into a thin book.

The real data is about that thinness. In the COVID crash the market makers and
the high-frequency firms withdrew, and sometimes 80% of the normal book was
missing. Nothing else in the corpus puts a number on what liquidity does when
it matters.

## The argument

**The case, made three ways.** First, evidence: read the footprint under a
chart low and the trades printing there are single-digit, so "what do the big
players want to fish for in the single-digit range?" Second, information:
everyone knows stops sit under extreme lows, but "how much liquidity is
actually there, nobody knows", and nobody knows where the stops are except
your broker. Third, behaviour: a large player builds a position over time,
through spreads, hedged with options, sometimes over weeks, and prefers to do
it where the market is fairly priced, inside a range. A one-minute dip below a
low serves none of that. The ICT school is named as the source of the story
that "the banks supposedly always sweep the stops".

**What happens instead.** Moves through the lows are put down to events and to
pain. Whoever has been long the whole time eventually feels it, runs out of
money "in quotation marks", and closes at market; the market turns aggressive
and new buyers step in because the price now looks nice. The host later adds
the mechanical half: a huge red candle does not mean a massive sell-off, it
means the liquidity was further down and got filled there, and "not much
liquidity was traded within that candle". That is, nearly line for line, the
Bookmap read Carmine Rosato gives at the 2 October 2024 low in [episode
4](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md): retail sell bubbles under a trendline, then a
column of large buys at wholesale. Carmine calls it "the classic stop hunt"
and buys it. This podcast describes the same tape, refuses the label, and
never says whether to buy it. The disagreement is about the agent and the
noun, not the tape.

**Where the corpus splits, and how far.** [Trading
Notes](../IUWvHVout94/CARD.md) opens on the identical thesis, "NOBODY IS
HUNTING YOUR STOPS", and then teaches identify the pool, wait for the sweep,
enter on confirmation. Smart Money Decode X goes further: [video
3](../7F9wBYse-Nk/CARD.md) answers its own question, "HUNTING YOUR STOPS? YES,
MECHANICALLY, THEY NEED YOUR LIQUIDITY", and [video 6](../GGe2widuvPs/CARD.md)
draws an "ENGINEERED PUSH BELOW SWING LOW". All three reject malice. The
podcast also rejects the mechanism: an institution that needed a stop cluster
to fill would print as size, and the size is not there; and nothing is
engineered, "the market is triggered by certain events". Two cautions before
weighing it in the podcast's favour. Thin prints under a low are also what
thin stops look like when they go. And Trading Notes' law, that predictable
behaviour produces predictable liquidity, needs only the half the guest
concedes: everyone knows where the stops are placed. The channel says the same
again in [aursfDVYzUk](../aursfDVYzUk/CARD.md), which dismisses "stop-loss
waves" and blames fast moves on institutional hedging.

**Support and resistance, nearer Carmine than the card records.** The card
files "in reality, they don't actually exist" against [Carmine's episode
5](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md). Read the whole answer and the two are close. The
guest's real level is price arriving, active trading, the level holding and
the market moving away, "because the market confirms it for me": Carmine's
validation-by-order-flow thesis. His further step is that an unconfirmed level
is no level at all, and that he prefers areas where the participants who
traded before are expected again; "if they're no longer there, then it's no
longer a support or resistance". That is the depletion rule of SMDX video 6,
each visit consumes orders, reached from the opposite camp.

**The numbers, and what kind they are.** The stressed-liquidity passage is the
reason to keep this video. When volatility passes a speed threshold the
algorithms and high-frequency firms, the biggest liquidity providers there
are, withdraw: sometimes 80% of the normal book is missing. It is the
corpus's only data on
execution costs in a crisis, and every depth assumption in the corpus was
built in calm markets. The other number is a scale reference: 10,000 ES
contracts being bought means "something big", though it could be one player or
10,000 traders. Against the "truly large" resting order of more than 250 to
300 contracts in [Vd83oo_geMk](../Vd83oo_geMk/CARD.md), these are different
quantities, resting size versus executed buying. None of the channel's

**Method, in fragments.** The guest tells himself a story, forms a subjective
opinion, and requires the market to confirm it, "and in doing so you become
objective": idea-and-proof from NkQeOVDTAec, and the confirmation leg of
Carmine's CLC rule. The host's one rule, trade in the direction where there is
more money, draws a caveat from the guest: if the market keeps creating
liquidity at a level and keeps rejecting it, someone may be building a
position there. That is absorption as [usho6UVLqkE](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md)
and Carmine's [episode 2](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md) define it, in tension with
the same channel's magnet idea. No entry, no stop, no trade, no statistic; and
the closing line is the honest one: what one of them calls market noise the
other calls support and resistance, and what one calls a ping-pong game the
other calls the flow.
