**What it teaches.** The fair value gap (FVG) as a three-candle pattern:
bullish when candle 3's low sits above candle 1's high (zone = C1 high →
C3 low), bearish when candle 3's high sits below candle 1's low (zone = C1
low → C3 high), the middle candle "significantly larger". The spoken
definition is explicitly wick to wick — *"a space between the wick of the
candle before it and the wick of the candle after it"* (00:48) — not the
candle bodies. It forms when a
concentration of aggressive orders consumes resting orders at several
levels at once, so price jumps and the skipped levels are the gap. It tends
to be revisited because the market "tends toward efficiency" and
participants who missed the move seek to interact there — but "context
decides", not the gap alone. Four significance factors (displacement size,
timeframe, structural context, fresh vs tested); order block + FVG as the
strongest confluence; a definition of "mitigated"; four mistakes; a
four-step framework whose last step is *wait for the reaction inside the
zone*. No stop, target, R-multiple, win rate or instrument; every chart is
an AI-drawn mock. Whiteboard slides + cartoon illustrations, same format as
videos 1–10. Spoken cross-reference: *"the order blocks you learned in video
12"* — playlist #12 (`T85mcZQnwK8`) is not in the corpus.

## Lessons (this video)
_(anything peculiar to this video/channel: layout, chart software, segment structure)_

- Same AI-slide format as videos 1–10, and the same hazard sharpened: **the
  drawings can contradict the rule they illustrate.** Every bullish diagram
  labels the zone's top and bottom the wrong way round (five slides,
  00:54–04:34); the 01:04 drawing has two candles labelled C3; the 10:08
  "SIGNIFICANT FVG" boxes cover whole pullbacks; the 01:40 order book is
  upside down; the 07:12 "LESS SIGNIFICANT" arrow points at the most
  significant bar; the 09:27 candles run A–E, E, G and its gauge says WEAKER
  at both ends. Slide *text* carries the content; treat every
  chart as decoration unless a number on it is read off in speech.

  "TradingView", "BTC/USDT 4H", "SPX500 from 18 Oct"; a zoom added

  against frames matched. What the zooms drop is the secondary layer —
  candle tags, axis words, bracket labels, icon captions — about a third of
  it, as on video 7; the verifier's "additional text not reported" pass is
  what recovers it.
