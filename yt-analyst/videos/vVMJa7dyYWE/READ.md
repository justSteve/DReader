## In brief

A webinar on reading a session's volume profile by its shape. Dale gives four:
the D, a balance with its heaviest volume near the middle; the P, heavy at the
top, meaning buyers; the b, heavy at the bottom, meaning sellers; and the thin
profile, a trend that moved too fast to build a centre at all. Each shape comes
with a plan for the next day: fade the edges of a D back to its point of
control, buy pullbacks into the volume of a P, sell pullbacks into the volume
of a b, and trade the small volume bumps inside a thin profile as the places
where the aggressive side added to its position.

The one hard rule is the one the Vorwald channel never gives: a profile that
looks like a P is not a P unless the day closes in the upper half of its range,
and the mirror for a b. Everything else is asserted. The 70% of the time the
market spends rotating, the institutional reading of wide volume, the 67% win
rate: none of it is tested on screen.

## The argument

Dale's alphabet is the same one the Vorwald channel calls PbD, and the two
arrive at it independently. Where the Vorwald presenter says the letters are
[balance phases and aggressive trend phases](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md), and the
reading of them as the shapes a distribution draws is our gloss on his words,
Dale says shapes and means it: the D-shaped profile "corresponds with the
letter D", the P "looks like letter P", and you may "need to use your
imagination a bit" to see which one is on the chart. He adds a fourth letter
the Vorwald material treats as a special case, the thin profile, and he gives
it his favourite setup.

The reading of each shape is conventional and clear. A D is a rotation; buyers and sellers "consider it a fair price". After a trend it
means the trend has paused or ended. A P forms either as a V-shaped rejection
of lower prices or as the heavy volume of an uptrend shifting higher, and in
either case "it means buyers". The b is the mirror. The thin profile is a trend
with "no pullbacks or very little pullbacks", often started by macro news, and
the reason it has no centre is that "there's simply no time for the big trading
institutions to place their huge orders there." That last line is the same
model of institutional behaviour the Vorwald channel states in its
[entry video](../aursfDVYzUk/CARD.md), where size is said to take a day and a
half to two days to route: institutions need rotation, so a profile without a
centre is a profile they did not build.

The frequency claim also matches. Dale says "I would say like maybe 70% of the
time the market is in a rotation", and the Vorwald channel puts balance at
about 70% in [its Market Profile video](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md) and in
[four others](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md). Neither sources it, and the corpus
cannot yet tell whether the number is a fact of the auction or a figure that
circulates in the profile literature.

How he trades the shapes is where the method becomes usable. Inside a D, the
edges are the trade and the point of control is the target: "short from the
higher border of the rotation and long from the bottom border". In a P or b,
the trade is a pullback to the point of control or to a smaller volume bump in
the thin part of the profile, and he is specific that the bump matters even
when its volume is modest, because it marks "a place where buyers stepped in
and started to buy aggressively". He has also changed his own entry: he used
to trade from the exact price of heaviest volume and now trades "from the
beginning of the heavy volume zone", because it misses fewer trades. This is
pullback trading throughout, never entry on the break, which puts him with
Carmine Rosato's [buy the pullback, never the break](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md)
and with the Vorwald channel's [pullback after breakout](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md)
entry.

There is a quiet disagreement with Carmine underneath the agreement. Dale's
volume bump is support because aggressive buyers built it. Carmine's
[support-and-resistance episode](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md) argues that a level
made by aggressive buying is a level waiting to break, and that real support is
aggression exhausting into passive size. They are reading the same footprint
and drawing opposite conclusions about who was in control of it. Dale never
looks at who was passive.

The 50% rule is the video's real contribution. Twice, on slides and in speech,
he shows a profile with a P's silhouette whose day closed below the midpoint of
its range and says: not a P, do not trade it as one. The Vorwald channel's PbD
videos have a [candle-close rule](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md) for confirming a
breakout; they have no rule for deciding whether the letter itself is valid.
Dale's rule is cheap to check and it is the only place in either channel where
the shape is tied to something the close can falsify.

The track-record claim deserves its own weight. Since 2016 he has published
almost 1,500 ideas on TradingView and says they show a 67% win rate "without
any money management, position management, nor any special rules". The
Vorwald channel's [80 to 90%](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md) is a number for one
setup with no risk stated; its [worked example](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) is a
50% hit rate and a risk-reward of maybe two. Dale's 67% is different in kind:
it points at a public archive anyone can score. It is also self-scored, on
ideas rather than fills, with no risk-reward, no drawdown and no sizing, so it
tells you how often his levels reacted, not what a trader would have made. His
[other card in this corpus](../8O_GIxjhLkc/CARD.md), a replayed ES short, is
the only place we have seen his numbers on a ticket, and it realised 1.22R.

**Trading the P, and why a small bump counts** *(16:22)*

> "The two most important places in a P-shaped profile is point of control and
> the little volume bump in the low volume zone. So let's take a look at this

> **Editor —** Here is the disagreement with Carmine Rosato in one sentence.

**The 50% rule** *(17:29)*

> "Now there is one thing that you need to know about the P-shaped profile, and
> one thing that you need to be careful about. There are two examples here.
> This P-shaped profile is actually not P-shaped profile, even though it does
> have a similarity to the letter P, but it's not a proper P-shaped profile.
> The other one also looks like a letter P, but this one is a proper P-shaped
> profile. The reason the first one is not a proper P-shaped profile is, if you
> look at the price, how the price closed: it closed below 50% mark, 50% of the
> daily range. So if this is the daily range, then the close of the day is
> below 50%. If the day closes like that, then this is not a proper P-shaped
> profile, because P-shaped profile is buyers, right? But this is not buyers.
> When day closes like that, it's not buyers. So when you see this, you don't
> trade it as a P-shaped profile. On the other hand, this one is a proper
> P-shaped profile, because if I draw the 50% mark here, the day closes above
> that 50% mark. So that's buyers. So keep this in mind: not everything that
> looks like a P profile actually is a P-shaped profile."

> **Editor —** The one rule in the video, and the one the Vorwald PbD material
> lacks. It is a close-location filter: the shape says what happened during the
> day, the close says who won it. Note it is a rule about the *letter*, not
> about the entry; he still has no stop, target or size anywhere in 38 minutes.
> The same rule appears in mirror at 23:51 for the b, where a day that closes
> above its midpoint is not a proper b.

> "Let's now go for the b-shaped profile. This one is easy, because it is the

**Where in the zone he enters** *(22:18)*

> "Now how do we trade the b-shaped profile? [...] If I extend the point of
> control to the next day, then this would be the place to go short from,
> right, because there was the point of control, price made a little pullback,
> and you go short from there. Then there's also this little volume bump here,
> and this also represents a strong resistance, and another short when the
> price makes pullback to it. Now, as you can see, I drawn the level from the
> beginning of that heavy volume zone. That's what I like to do. I currently

> **Editor —** A real, if small, method detail the card does not carry: the

**The thin profile** *(24:41)*

> "Now let's talk about the last main shape of volume profile, and it is the
> thin profile. It looks like this: it is thin, and the reason it is thin is
> that it forms when there is a strong trend, very strong trend, usually no
> pullbacks or very little pullbacks, and very often those strong trends are
> initiated by macro news. And there's no central heavy volume zone here. The
> reason is that when there is a strong trend like this, there's simply no time
> for the big guys, for the big trading institutions, to place their huge
> orders there. They need rotation, not a strong trend like this. So that's why
> when the price shoots in one direction like this, there are not really any
> major heavy volume zones there. There are only those smaller volume bumps.
> [...] What it is telling us about the market is simply that one side of the
> market, either buyers or sellers, are absolutely dominating that market."

> "Now the most important places in a thin profile are the volume bumps. [...]
> They are showing us places where strong buyers, who are pushing the price
> upwards, were adding aggressively to the long positions. So that's why those
> work as strong supports. [...] The price likes to react to those heavy volume
> bumps, because those are aggressive buyers who are helping to push the price
> upwards."

> **Editor —** "They need rotation" is the institutional model the Vorwald
> channel also holds: size takes time to route, so it can only be placed where
> price lingers. On that model a thin profile is evidence of absence, and the
> bumps inside it are the only footprints available. It is a coherent story;
> nothing in the video tests it.

> **Editor —** The slide reads "Thin profile + broken swing point". Two
> independent reasons for one level is the same architecture as the Vorwald
