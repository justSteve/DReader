## In brief

Seven videos on this channel run on the PbD logic; this is the one that says
what the letters are. Near the end, almost as an aside: the D's are large
balance phases, the P's the aggressive trend phases upwards, the b's the
aggressive trend phases downwards. On a Market Profile that is a picture. A
rally driven hard and then accepted near its highs draws a capital P, thin
stem and fat top; a sell-off accepted at its lows draws a lowercase b; a
market agreeing on price draws the bell, a D.

Underneath is a claim about who does the driving. A market pushed aggressively
one way was pushed by participants in distress, hedging or reacting to
something fundamental, and the balance that follows is those participants
accepting the new price. The letter tells you who is dominant and where they
are comfortable. Then plan two things: what happens if it holds, and what
happens if it fails.

## The argument

He defines the logic as a way to see at a glance where a market is in balance
and where it is not, where the global players are in their comfort zone. Then
the step that makes it order-flow reasoning. If participants have driven a
market very aggressively one way, they were usually in distress, whether that
was hedging or a fundamental reaction that led to hedging. He does not care
which: we will not ask what news caused the move, only that it happened. It is
the same account of institutional flow as
[NkQeOVDTAec](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md), where large participants must route
orders, and the "forced to buy" of [jXR1afMy-3o](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md); and
it is Carmine Rosato's opening premise
([QaNPAaEnB5E](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md)): a market event creates an imbalance
and a new fair value forms. Neither man cites the other.

The first example is a P, which he never names. On ES, a TPO profile over a
volume profile, the market opens, dips, rallies aggressively, reacts and goes
sideways. Two single prints in the stem say a major participant reacted at
once because they had to; the balance above says they held. His single print
has a clock on it: a price range where the market was never for longer than 30
minutes. That is one 30-minute block of the profile from
[K8qtT2_axPo](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md), visited once and left, sharper than the
"swift movement" of [1utSjHs_Mq8](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md). The close sits high
and the next day opens and shifts its balance up. The participants who played
here, he says, had an idea, and it was that the price will rise.

The second example is the b, and here he says the letter. Gold sells off
aggressively and goes sideways; the sideways phase is acceptance of lower
prices. He draws the volume profile freehand beside it: low, high, low, more,
nothing at the bottom. The thin band between the nodes is unfair price traded
aggressively in a short sequence, so the seller was dominant and still holds
the upper hand, needing these prices. That, he says, is a b. Note when he says
it: at the moment the lower balance forms, not at the sell-off. The letter
names the trend phase; the formation needs the balance that accepted it.

Then the discipline: always plan two things, because the whole thing can go
wrong. For the P: if the market goes back below the equilibrium and forms
acceptance, there is a high probability it clears out the entire trend
downwards. For the b: acceptance and continuation lower is the first option;
if instead the market says no, we do not accept these prices any more, and
moves up, it will with high probability run past the start of the move and on
to the next fair price area. [jXR1afMy-3o](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md) states the
mirror, a failed P targeting below the start of its impulse;
[HySZZSjMxF8](../HySZZSjMxF8/CARD.md) enters that failed break on
re-acceptance; Carmine's ep. 6 ([w7tvJCuZAq8](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md)) looks
for the reversal first on almost every breakout.

Where the failed letter goes is the second contribution. The market moves from
fair price to fair price to conduct its business there; large traders need
liquidity, and where liquidity was, they assume it is again. That is
liquidity-seeking with the malice taken out, consistent with the podcast that
calls stop-hunting a myth yet concedes liquidity further down gets filled
([RwQBdF9TSvc](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md)) and with Trading Notes' "NOBODY IS
HUNTING YOUR STOPS" ([IUWvHVout94](../IUWvHVout94/CARD.md)). It is one
instinct with the full-profile traverse of
[1utSjHs_Mq8](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md), the return to fair price of
[YmygDgtoxO8](../YmygDgtoxO8/CARD.md) and Carmine's opposing-level target.

Now the expansion, with care, because the corpus rests on it. What he says is
phases: D balance, P aggressive trend up, b aggressive trend down. He does not
say the letters are shapes. The shape reading stands on what he points at
here, on [Ly62G168MkQ](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md), which draws P and b by hand
and names a P-profile as impulse then consolidation near the highs, on the D
setup of [jXR1afMy-3o](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md), "a profile that the market has
formed, which looks like this", and on the b-structure and P-waves of
[Kg6sYKgtkrY](../Kg6sYKgtkrY/CARD.md). It holds, as a reading of the corpus
rather than a sentence in this video. His last word on method is that every
rule derives from market profiling and the closing price count, the close rule
of [jXR1afMy-3o](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md) and
[Ly62G168MkQ](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md); the counting rules stay unstated.

What it is not. Nothing is crisis-specific beyond the word "distress", and
there is no entry, stop, target distance, risk-reward, hit rate or trade. The
opening calls him the champion maker over a leaderboard on which Patrick
Nill's 202.1% is not the largest figure (Pau Perdices Bellet shows 600.9%),
and a separate slide puts a 3 beside Christoph Radecker's 47.7%. Where a rank
is legible for Nill's figure, in [0CrkbfuIhkc](../0CrkbfuIhkc/CARD.md), it is
a 2. No slide in the sweep shows a 1 beside either name.
