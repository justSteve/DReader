# Read: 90% of Traders Use VWAP WRONG! (Here's How to Do It Right)

> **The channel's answer to when VWAP helps and when it hurts: three uses for one line, each valid only in the regime it belongs to, and the market's structure classified before any of them.**

## In brief

He is arguing that VWAP fails traders not because it is a bad line but because
they read it without asking what kind of day it is. The line is nothing more
than today's volume-weighted average price, reset every session, and it
behaves one way in a trend and the opposite way in a range. Since markets
trend only about 30% of the time, anyone who fades every stretch away from
VWAP is fading a trend on the days it matters most. His fix is a gate before
the tool: read the open, decide whether the day is trending or balanced, and
only then choose among three uses. In a trend, buy the pullback to VWAP. If
you fade an extended move, treat VWAP as a target that keeps moving. When VWAP
runs flat, the market is in balance, and you work the range edges with VWAP as
a modest target. For us this is the switching rule the channel's two entry
videos never state.

## The argument

He starts by deflating the indicator. Most people read VWAP as a fair-price
gauge, above fair, below fair, in between, and "here lies the biggest problem
with the whole story". VWAP shows nothing other than the volume-weighted
average price of today. It looks like a moving average, it is recalculated
every single day, and when one day ends "it starts again essentially at
zero"; "usually", he hedges, it is set for the current day. The study bar on
his chart shows variance bands at 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2, the only VWAP band
settings in the corpus, and he never refers to them.

The core of the video is a frequency claim. Markets are "only about 30% truly
stably trending", and about 70% they are not. That is the channel's standing
split: 70% balance in the [market-profile lecture](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md),
70% return to fair value in the [volume-profile video](../YmygDgtoxO8/CARD.md),
and the 30% regime the [80 to 90% win-rate video](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md)
confines its breakout to. In the language of the [crisis-PbD video](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md), where
D is a balance phase and P and b are aggressive trend phases, a flat VWAP is
the line through a D and a steep one the line through a P or a b, though he
never says so.

From the split follows the failure mode. If VWAP were simply a counter-trend
tool used on every day, "then I'd have 7 million stop losses here". So the
first rule is that the VWAP "must be based on a trend" before he will trade off
it. Institutions, he says, read the line as a bias, bullish above and bearish
below, "but again, without context, you'll just keep getting punched in the
nose". Context means deciding whether the sequence ahead is a trend or a
sideways-weighted market, and the help he offers, "for example", is the open:
if the open says the day is trendy and VWAP is running alongside, the
trend-continuation use is on. This is Carmine Rosato's order in his
[entry and exit episode](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md), context before location
before confirmation, and the presenter nearly says so: with the open and the
VWAP in hand, "what I don't have yet is a corresponding setup". VWAP is "a
benchmark, a context provider", not a trigger.

Then he separates three traders who might look at the same line. The counter
trader waits until the market "shows me no more liquidity" at an extreme and
uses VWAP as a profit target that keeps moving up with the trend. The trend
follower waits for the pullback to VWAP, having first asked whether the move
has already hit target areas that collapsed it. The two trade "a completely
different location", one from above toward the line, one from the line
upward. That pullback is the entry Carmine's
[volume episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md) insists on, buy the pullback and
never the break, with VWAP here as the reference rather than the tape.

The third trader is the scalper. When VWAP runs sideways "you can already see
balance", and the scalper sells the upper price ranges and buys the lower
ones. He is honest about the economics: taking the trade only to VWAP gives
"very modest profit targets", and the model applies "only if the VWAP is
currently oriented sideways, and I can't assume a maximally brutal breakout
now or a trend day from the market open". Once VWAP starts sloping, the
scalper's edge is gone.

The passage worth the most to us is a sentence in the recap. Because a moving
VWAP target will not let you fix a risk-reward in advance, "you have to know
your hit rate, and then through your hit rate and the minimum usable
risk-reward ratio, you can know, 'I have to trade at least up to here,
otherwise the whole thing makes no sense'". That is the win-rate identity his
[scalping video](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md) applies to a bad-location trade,
stated here as a planning principle, and it is the frame his
[statistics video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) fills with a worked example at a 50%
hit rate and a risk-reward of "maybe a two". This video supplies no figures of
its own.

Where it sits in the corpus. His [companion VWAP video](../VumVuGnCcFM/CARD.md)
restates the rule, mean reversion only when structure is sideways and
balanced, and adds the warning this one lacks: a trader may win twenty times
fading toward VWAP and be liquidated on the twenty-first. And it is the
missing piece for the [scalping video](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md), whose
expansion entry looked like a contradiction of the reversion trade taught
elsewhere on the channel. The selection rule exists: classify the structure
first. What survives is that neither entry video says so. One caution, ours:
the card compresses this into two modes, but he gives three, and the counter
trader's fade to a steep VWAP lives in the trend regime, not in balance.
