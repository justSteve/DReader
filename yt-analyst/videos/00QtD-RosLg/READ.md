# Read: Everything you've learned about trading is wrong...

> **Carmine Rosato's own compression of his eight-episode series: auction, absorption, fade the breakout, read the tape in "the now" — and in 26 minutes of summary, still not one number a trader could set a rule on.**

- **Channel:** Carmine Rosato — Trading Orderflow Series, episode 8 (final) · **Uploaded:** 2024-11-25 · **Duration:** 26:24
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=00QtD-RosLg

## In brief

The last episode teaches nothing new; it tells you what the author thinks the
series was. He spends the longest stretch on the premise, that a market is an
auction and a candle is only a picture of one, and the shortest on the tooling:
the DOM that had an episode of its own is gone, and the heatmap survives as a
single trade. The core, in his words, is "spotting absorption or spotting
trapped traders", and the method built on it has hardened in the retelling.
Episode 6 hedged its reversal-first rule to roughly 99% of the time in the
voice-over; the recap keeps only the slide's word, "always". The one fresh
framing is a question: who
is in control of price? Most losing trades, he says, are shorts taken while
buyers control it or longs while sellers do, and a candle cannot tell you
which. What the recap drops is as telling as what it keeps: break-and-retest,
the continuation framework, the cost of waiting for a candle close, and every
number that would make the method testable.

## The argument

**How he weights it.** A recap is a map of what its author thinks mattered,
and this map is lopsided. Measured off the captions, the auction premise from
[episode 1](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md) (the $400,000 house, Apple at $300 and then
$400, the open-outcry pit) runs more than a fifth of the video, with the funnel
straight after it: every strategy, indicator or algorithm ends as a buy or sell
order. [Episode 3](../tgQC7Dpcc8A/CARD.md), the longest in the series and the
one that taught the DOM and the Bookmap heatmap, gets about a minute, and the
DOM is not taught at all; "depth of market" is spoken once.

**The one new framing.** Rallying into supply, he asks who is in control of
price. The rule that follows is on no earlier slide: the majority of losing
trades are shorts taken while buyers are in control or longs taken while
sellers are, so short the supply box only if sellers are strong there and
otherwise pass. A candlestick "only tells you where price was and where price
currently is." It is the same question the Vorwald channel uses to define
order flow in the first place, who is currently buying, who is currently
selling, and how aggressively ([vl01TiVTuoQ](../vl01TiVTuoQ/CARD.md)).

**What has hardened.** Absorption is "the number one most important concept
to my trading". The reasoning is still the auction: many aggressive buyers and no higher prices
means passive supply exceeds demand, and "the market has only one option."
That is the same read as absorption in the Vorwald sweep
([usho6UVLqkE](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md)). But the fade thesis has lost its
hedge: [episode 6](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md)'s slide said always and its
voice-over said roughly 99% of the time on the first test; the recap keeps
only "always". And two frameworks vanish. Episode 6's continuation method (confirm strong volume, then buy the
pullback that holds) is not mentioned, and neither is
[episode 5](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md)'s break-and-retest. The recap is a pure
reversal method, so a reader of it alone would never see that Carmine's retest
entry and the Vorwald pullback-after-breakout
([NkQeOVDTAec](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md)) are the same trade.

**The mechanism, stated better than before.** He disowns "smart money" as a
term, substitutes patient versus impatient traders, and gives the clearest
version yet of [episode 4](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md)'s bait-fish idea: "Retail and
big money are the auction. Big money has a need to stay ahead of retail for
their orders to be filled. They have to buy when they want retail to sell." A
break of support is retail "selling into other traders' buy orders." That puts
him between two positions in the corpus. The Vorwald channel calls
stop-hunting "a retail trading myth" and points at single-digit prints below
the lows ([RwQBdF9TSvc](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md)); Trading Notes writes NOBODY
IS HUNTING YOUR STOPS on screen and trades the sweep anyway
([IUWvHVout94](../IUWvHVout94/CARD.md)). Carmine's version needs no hunter:
the size has to be filled by somebody, and the somebody is whoever is
stopping out.

**The level is downstream of the orders.** On episode 5's five-touch
resistance: "The orders influence why resistance forms; resistance doesn't
influence why the orders are there." A better statement than episode 5 made,
and closer than episode 5 sounded to the Vorwald claim that fixed support and
resistance "do not truly exist". Both put the line after the flow; they
differ only on whether to draw it.

**CLC and the now.** Context, location, confirmation returns with context
shrunk to "when the volume is strong", then the tightest sentence in the
series: "If there's no answer, that's an answer in itself." Entry is when the
seller gets filled, never after the dump; the best setups "cannot be seen on a
candlestick chart." [Episode 7](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md) priced that claim, a
2-point stop on the print against roughly 7 points after the five-minute
close; the recap keeps the slogan and drops the price. It is also the series'
standing dispute with the Vorwald channel and the Smart Money Decode X series,
both of which want a close, not a wick.

**The gap the recap does not close.** He opens promising "5 to 1, 10 to 1"
risk-reward and "what I do to exit the trade when my target hits." He
delivers two trades, both $21,750 winners from earlier episodes, and one
sentence on exits: "I took profits along the way." No delta or volume
threshold, no sizing (the series' "30 contracts" is not even repeated), no
exit rule, no win rate. The series ends with seven journaled winners and no
losers, a curated set rather than a record. The Vorwald channel does supply a
sample size of 200 to 300 trades, a worked example at a 50% hit rate, and an
average risk-reward of "maybe a two." Carmine's last word on the subject is
the same as his first: none.

## In his words

**Who is in control of price** *(07:13)*

> "The majority of the losing trades come from if you're shorting the market
> and buyers are in control, or if you're buying the market and sellers are in
> control... If sellers are strong, I'm going to short it. If buyers are strong,
> I am not going to short it, I'm going to avoid taking this setup. By viewing a
> candlestick chart I cannot tell who is strictly and solely in control."

> **Editor —** The one slide in the recap that is not a rerun, per the card.
> It is episode 7's confirmation pillar restated as a filter on losses rather
> than a trigger for entries, which is the more useful form.

**Absorption, the number-one concept** *(10:22)*

> "The number one most important concept to my trading is spotting absorption
> or spotting trapped traders. I don't want to call it my holy grail, but this
> is what I look for on a daily basis... If you see a lot of buyers pouring
> into the market but the market's not going up higher, that means the supply
> is greater than the demand... The market has only one option, and that will
> be to move down... When I see selling I'm first looking to buy the market."

> **Editor —** "First looking to" is a bias, not an entry; the entry is the
> confirmation below. What he never gives, here or anywhere in the series, is
> how much aggression counts as "a lot".

**The Oct 3 trade** *(12:41)*

> "Here we can see a negative delta of −735, another negative delta of −1171.
> On top of this the cumulative delta bars down here are the highest amount of
> selling as the market is breaking the low of day... Theoretically the market
> should continue to sell off. Guess what, it does not continue to sell off.
> This chart right here looks extremely bearish, right? Nothing here looks
> bullish."

> **Editor —** He introduces it as "$21,000 profit", "9 to 1", "I risked about
> $2,200". The log on screen reads $21,750 and $2,250, which is 9.67R;
> [episode 2](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md) and episode 7 carry the verified figures.
> It is the trade the series shows most often, and like the other six it
> journals, it won.

**The large buyer on the heatmap** *(13:52)*

> "We could see a positive delta... of 4,000, or a large green bubble, which
> indicates large aggressive buying at 5812... Put yourself in the shoes of
> that large buyer. You'd want to see the market defend prices, defend him...
> Here we could see the market sell off into him. The delta gets smaller, which
> means he is getting filled, and the market's trying to move down but it's not
> moving down."

> **Editor —** Episode 3's Oct 9 trade, and all the recap keeps of the heatmap. The caption renders its risk as "$55,000"; the episode 3 log reads
> $5,250, which against the same $21,750 result is the "4 to 1" he quotes.
> Episode 3's card places the +4,000 print at 5810 with 5812 as the target,
> and records the delta as spoken, never pixel-verified. The heatmap itself is
> the corpus's open dispute: the Vorwald channel says institutions do not rely
> on one ([Vd83oo_geMk](../Vd83oo_geMk/CARD.md)).

**Patient versus impatient** *(15:48)*

> "Retail and big money are the auction. Big money has a need to stay ahead of
> retail for their orders to be filled. They have to buy when they want retail
> to sell, and they have to sell when they want retail to buy. The support
> level breaks, you short the support level, you are selling into other
> traders' buy orders."

> **Editor —** The mechanism behind episode 4's bait fish, and the cleanest
> statement of it in the series. He also says "95% of retail traders lose",
> twice; episode 5's slide said 90%, and neither figure is ever sourced.

**Why a level is good** *(18:19)*

> "The orders influence why resistance forms; resistance doesn't influence why
> the orders are there... You see all of this buying and people will say, hey,
> there's tons of blue here, I should be longing this. Well, you have to ask
> yourself who is on the other side of the trade. If everybody is longing
> this, somebody has to be selling it."

> **Editor —** Belongs to episode 5, and beats it.

**Reversal first, now without the hedge** *(20:14)*

> "If you buy every breakout you are positioning yourself to become trapped by
> smart money and you are giving them your liquidity. I am always looking for
> reversals on breakouts... Strong aggressive buying volume with no upside
> follow through is a reversal signal where I am first looking to sell the
> market."

> **Editor —** Episode 6's slide also said always; its voice-over hedged to
> roughly 99% of the time on the first test. The recap keeps the slide and
> drops the hedge. Missing beside it: episode 6's continuation
> framework and episode 5's break-and-retest, the two places the series told
> you how to trade with a break rather than against it.

**The now, and the answer that is no answer** *(23:20)*

> "The only way to do this is focus in the now... Not focusing on a time but
> focusing on what the market's presenting me right now, via the order flow,
> via the volume, via the tape. This is when I get all the answers that I need.
> And if there's no answer, that's an answer in itself, because that's telling
> me to sit sidelined... Wouldn't it make sense to get short as soon as we see
> the seller get filled? It would not make sense to wait for the market to dump
> to get short... The best setups come from ones that cannot be seen on a
> candlestick chart...
> As it's breaking above the high of day, 5044, 5046, 5047, 5048, we could see
> the time and sales here. Here we see a seller present. So wouldn't it make
> sense to get short when the seller is present here, versus getting short
> when the market dumps and starts moving lower?... I want to get in before the
> move, which maximizes my reward and minimizes my risk."

> **Editor —** Episode 7 priced this: about 2 points of risk on the print
> against about 7 after the five-minute close, same target. The recap keeps
> the principle and drops the arithmetic. It is also where he and the Vorwald
> channel part for good: that channel wants a close, not a wick. And note what
> never arrives: the exit rule promised in the opening minute. "I took profits
> along the way" is all the series ever says.
