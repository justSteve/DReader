# Read: Swing Highs and Swing Lows — How to Identify Them Correctly (Video 8)

> **The series' first hard rules: a swing point is qualified by counting the candles on each side that failed to exceed it. One is noise, three starts to mean something, five or more is structure, and a daily screen should carry no more than five or six of them.**

- **Channel:** Smart Money Decode X · Zero to Advanced Trader, video 8 · **Uploaded:** 2026-08-21 · **Duration:** 12:25
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2RzSJC98o8

## In brief

Seven videos into a series that had not yet given a rule you could set on a chart, this one gives a dozen. The problem is familiar: mark every reversal as a swing point and the chart becomes fog, because "when everything looks like a swing point, nothing is a swing point." The fix is a count. A swing high is a candle higher than its neighbours, but it only *matters* in proportion to how many candles on each side failed to exceed it. One candle each side is noise. Three is starting to mean something. Five or more is a structural turning point, and on a daily chart, ten candles each side is ten days of rejection.

From that count come three tiers, per-timeframe minimums, a density cap, and the claim the rest of the series rests on: swing points are primary evidence of where buyers and sellers decided, and their spacing reads trend health better than any indicator. The method works unchanged on ES.

## The argument

The video opens with a detective who cannot mark every footprint at a scene and expect to learn anything. Swing highs and lows are the footprints that matter, and mis-identifying them is "one of the most common and most invisible mistakes in all of retail trading." Invisible, because the trader who marks the wrong swing cannot see the error; everything built on top simply misbehaves.

He starts with the mechanical definition and immediately declares it insufficient. A swing high is a candle whose high exceeds the highs immediately left and right. In a choppy market almost every candle passes that test, so what is needed is a *qualified* swing high, and the qualifier is the number of candles on each side with a lower high. More candles, more significance. His analogy is a cliff face: every wave leaves a mark, but the tide mark is the one that "required the most sustained effort to reach and the most sustained resistance to stop at." The swing low is the same logic inverted, with its own image: a puddle in a parking lot is a low point, a valley between two mountains is a meaningful one.

The count then becomes a scale. Minor swings, three or fewer candles each side, are the internal structure of [video 7](../dAlP8UZXT48/CARD.md), useful for entry timing on lower timeframes and useless for anchoring a directional view. Intermediate swings, three to five, are what most traders use for 4H and daily structure and where the series' bullish, bearish, BOS and ChoCH labels are supposed to be applied. Major swings, five or more, anchor the highest-timeframe analysis and are, in his account, where the most significant liquidity pools sit: "most likely to be targeted by sweeps" and "most likely to hold when retested." Video 7 had asked only for "2+ lower highs each side"; this video is the refinement of that single rule into a graded one.

The practical procedure has four steps. Pick the analysis timeframe first, so that daily analysis uses daily swings. Scan left to right for turning points rather than patterns. Apply a minimum candle rule: two each side on the 4H, three on the daily. Then weigh each swing by what preceded it, because a reversal after a sustained move means more than one in the middle of a range. Three mistakes follow: too many swing points ("structural fog", with a cap of five or six per daily screen), swings borrowed from the wrong timeframe ("using a ruler to measure the distance between cities"), and treating every swing as equal weight when the size of the move and the number of candles it held make them anything but.

The passage that earns the video its place is the last third. Correct swing points, he argues, do not just define structure; they tell a story. Swings spreading further apart in price mean momentum building. Highs barely exceeding the prior high and lows barely holding mean buyers "working harder for less" and a trend approaching exhaustion, which "may not reverse immediately, but the warning is written in the progressively weakening swing points." No indicator can show this because indicators are derivatives computed after the fact; swing points are the primary record. This is the same instinct Carmine Rosato states in [his foundation episode](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md), where indicators and concepts are downstream of the auction, though Carmine's primary record is the order flow itself, not the price extremes it leaves behind.

That difference is the real cross-school seam. On liquidity, this video and Carmine's [premise episode](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md) agree almost word for word: stops cluster beyond obvious swings, and that is what gets swept. The mechanism was laid out in this channel's [video 6](../GGe2widuvPs/CARD.md). The Vorwald channel's [order-flow interview](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md) disputes the institutional agency in that story, pointing to single-digit prints under the lows, while conceding the mechanical effect of stops being triggered. On levels, the disagreement is sharper and worth holding onto. This video says a major swing is "most likely to hold when retested" because it held for many candles the first time. Carmine's [support-and-resistance episode](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md) says a level is only real when the order flow at the retest shows aggression exhausting into passive size, and a level that merely looks strong on a candle chart is the one he expects to break. The candle count tells you where to look; it does not tell you whether the level will hold today.

Where the video is loose, it is loose in the way the whole series is. The tiers overlap at three and at five, the 4H minimum of two candles is stated for "intermediate" swings the tier table defines as three to five, and the "max five or six" slide draws seven. A trader who wants a mechanical rule will have to pick the boundaries himself. The BOS and ChoCH questions the video closes on are answered in [video 9](../iPDi9_nzn-o/CARD.md) and [video 10](../YwqfGuktXrc/CARD.md), and the timeframe stack it assumes is [video 5's](../EW_TtV_rgzY/CARD.md).

## In his words

**Which footprints matter** *(00:02)*

Imagine you are a detective. You arrive at a scene and you need to identify which footprints matter and which ones are just noise. You could mark every single footprint on the ground, but that would give you hundreds of data points and no clarity. Or you could identify the significant footprints, the ones that tell you where someone actually walked, where they stopped, and where they turned around. That is exactly what identifying swing highs and swing lows is. You are not marking every candle that reversed. You are identifying the significant turning points that tell you the real story of the market.

And getting this wrong is one of the most common and most invisible mistakes in all of retail trading. Because you cannot read market structure without first identifying the right swing points. And if your swing points are wrong, everything built on them is wrong, too. [...] Every small reversal looks like it could be a swing high or swing low. And when everything looks like a swing point, nothing is a swing point. Because significance requires contrast.

**The definition, and why it is not enough** *(01:24)*

A swing high is a candle whose high is higher than the high of the candles immediately to its left and immediately to its right. That is the basic definition, but the basic definition is not enough for practical trading. Because in that definition, almost every candle in a choppy market qualifies as a swing high.

What we need is a qualified swing high. A swing high that is significant enough to be a real structural turning point. The way to qualify a swing high is by the number of candles on each side that have a lower high. The more candles on each side with a lower high, the more significant the swing high.

A swing high with one candle on each side is barely a swing at all. It is likely just noise within a range. A swing high with three candles on each side with lower highs is starting to mean something. A swing high with five or more candles on each side is a significant structural turning point. It means price went up to that level, held there while multiple time periods passed without price exceeding it, and then reversed lower. That kind of swing high represents a real decision point where sellers were in control for an extended period.

**The tide mark** *(02:37)*

Think about water marks on a cliff face. Every small wave leaves a mark, but those small marks are not meaningful. The meaningful mark is the one left by the highest wave before the tide turned. The one that required the most sustained effort to reach and the most sustained resistance to stop at. That is a significant swing high. The one that tells you something real happened there. Not every ripple, the tide mark.

**The swing low** *(03:08)*

A swing low follows the same logic in reverse. A candle whose low is lower than the lows of the candles immediately to its left and immediately to its right, qualified by the number of candles on each side with a higher low. [...] A swing low with five or more candles on each side telling you price reached that level, held while multiple periods passed without going lower, and then reversed higher. That is a real structural turning point. Buyers were present at that level with enough conviction to hold price above it for an extended period before it moved higher.

The analogy here is a valley between two mountains. A puddle in a parking lot is a low point, but it is not meaningful. It is just the result of rain filling the nearest depression. The valley between two significant peaks is a meaningful low point. It represents the place where the terrain stopped descending and began ascending again. Structural significance requires context. Not just the lowest point you can find. The lowest point that represents a real change in directional momentum.

**Three levels of significance** *(04:18)*

Not all swing points are equal, and treating them as equal is a mistake that leads to cluttered analysis and bad trades. There are three levels of swing point significance that every trader needs to understand.

The first level is the minor swing point. Three or fewer candles on each side. These are the internal structure swing points we discussed in video seven. They matter for entry timing and short-term analysis on the lower time frames, but they are not significant enough to anchor your directional analysis on the higher time frames. A trader who uses minor swing points to determine the overall market structure on the daily chart will constantly be confused because minor swings form and break every few hours.

The second level is the intermediate swing point. Three to five candles on each side. These are the swing points most traders use for 4-hour and daily structure analysis. They are significant enough to represent real turning points in the market. When an intermediate swing high holds for three to five candles on each side, it means the market genuinely paused and reversed at that level. These are the swing points you use to define bullish structure, bearish structure, break of structure, and change of character on your primary analysis time frame.

The third level is the major swing point. Five or more candles on each side. These are the swing points that anchor the highest time frame analysis. They represent the most significant turning points in a market over weeks or months. On the daily chart, a major swing high with 10 candles on each side represents a level where price was rejected for 10 full days before reversing. That is not noise. That is institutional decision-making happening in real time. Major swing points on the daily and weekly chart are the levels where the most significant liquidity pools sit. They are the levels most likely to be targeted by sweeps, and they are the levels most likely to hold when retested.

**Four steps, in real time** *(06:19)*

Step one is to choose your analysis time frame. If you are doing daily analysis, you need daily swing points. If you are looking at the 4-hour chart for setups, you need 4-hour swing points. The time frame determines which level of swing significance you are looking for.

Step two is to scan from left to right chronologically. You are not looking for patterns. You are looking for turning points. Moments where price changed direction, and that direction held for multiple candles before the next turn.

Step three is to apply the minimum candle rule. For intermediate swing points on the 4-hour chart, require at least two candles on each side with a lower high or higher low. For daily swing points, require at least three.

Step four is to verify with price action context. A swing high is more significant if it formed after a strong upward move. A swing low is more significant if it formed after a strong downward move. A reversal at a level after a sustained move in one direction is more meaningful than a reversal in the middle of a choppy range.

**Three mistakes** *(07:29)*

The first mistake is marking too many swing points. This is the most common mistake, and it creates what I call structural fog. [...] You cannot determine structure because every minor fluctuation is labeled as a significant swing. The rule is ruthless simplification. If you look at your chart and you see more than five or six swing points on a screen's worth of data on the daily chart, you are marking too many. Remove the ones with fewer candles on each side and keep only the most significant.

The second mistake is using swing points from the wrong time frame for your analysis. Using 1-hour swing points to determine daily structure is like using a ruler to measure the distance between cities. The tool is wrong for the job. Daily structure requires daily swing points. 4-hour setups require 4-hour swing points. 1-hour entries require 1-hour swing points. Match your swing point time frame to your analysis purpose.

The third mistake is treating all swing highs equally. A swing high that formed after a [...] move and held for eight candles on each side is fundamentally more significant than a swing high that formed after a [...] move and held for two candles on each side. They are both swing highs in the technical definition. But they carry completely different weight. When price returns to the major swing high, the reaction will be much more powerful than when it returns to the minor one. Weighting your swing points by significance, not just by definition, is what separates institutional-level structural analysis from retail-level structural analysis.

> **Editor —** The elided figures are forex pip counts for the size of the two moves; on ES, read the rule as the size of the move in points relative to the swings around it. The "five or six" cap is illustrated on the slide with a chart that marks seven.

**The story swing points tell** *(09:13)*

Once you can identify swing points correctly, they do not just define structure. They tell you a story. A bullish trend with swing highs and swing lows that are getting progressively further apart in terms of price range tells you momentum is building. Each new high is made with more force than the last. Each new low holds higher than it had to. The buyers are getting stronger with each swing.

A bullish trend where the swing highs are getting harder to make, where the highs are barely above the previous high, and where the swing lows are barely holding above the previous low, tells you a different story. Momentum is weakening. The buyers are having to work harder for each new high, and they are barely maintaining the lows. That trend is approaching exhaustion. It may not reverse immediately, but the warning is written in the progressively weakening swing points.

This is something no indicator can tell you, because indicators are derivatives of price. They are calculations applied after the fact. But swing points are primary. They are the direct evidence of where buyers and sellers made their decisions. And the quality of those decisions, visible in the size and spacing of swing points, tells you the health of the trend in real time.

> **Editor —** This is the video's best passage and its most contestable one. The claim that swing points are "primary" holds against indicators; it does not hold against the order flow, which is what produced the swing. Carmine's footprint reads of aggression that fails to move price are a more primary record of the same decisions, and can be read before the swing has finished forming.

**The atom** *(10:37)*

Every concept from this point in the series connects back to swing points. Liquidity pools sit at obvious swing highs and swing lows. That is where the stop-losses are. Order blocks form at the candle right before a swing point is created. That is where the institutional buying or selling originated. Fair value gaps appear within the moves between swing points. Volume analysis confirms whether the swing point was formed with genuine participation. And market structure, which we covered last video, is simply the sequence of qualified swing points telling you who is in control.

When you get swing point identification right, everything else falls into place. When you get it wrong, every concept built on it becomes unreliable. The swing point is the atom. Everything in the series is built from it.

**The mindset shift** *(11:28)*

Stop looking at your chart and thinking about trades. Start looking at it and mapping turning points. Before you consider a single entry, mark the significant swing highs and swing lows. Connect them. Read the story they tell. Is momentum building or weakening? Are the swing points getting larger or smaller? Is the most recent swing point a break of structure or a change of character? Those questions, answered from correctly identified swing points, give you a reading of the market that is more accurate than any indicator combination you will ever apply. Cuz you are reading the market in its own language, not through the distorted lens of a derived calculation.
