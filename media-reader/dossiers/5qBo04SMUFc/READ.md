# Read: How To Use Support and Resistance the RIGHT Way

> **A level is only support or resistance if the order flow shows aggression exhausting into passive size; a bounce made by aggressive buyers is a level waiting to break.**

- **Channel:** Carmine Rosato — Trading Orderflow Series, ep. 5 · **Uploaded:** 2024-11-08 · **Duration:** 29:21
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5qBo04SMUFc

## In brief

Carmine Rosato takes the most-taught idea in technical analysis and asks the
question almost nobody asks about it: why does a level hold? His answer comes
from auction theory. Resistance forms where the last buyer buys and support
where the last seller sells, and what stops price is not aggressive selling
but buying that runs out against passive sellers. On a footprint chart that
leaves a signature: prints thinning to a volume tail at the extreme, or a
large delta absorbed with nothing to show for it. A level that bounced
because aggressive buyers stepped in carries no such signature, and it is a
level he expects to break.

From that he draws three ways to trade a level, all of them built around who
is trapped, and shows one journaled ES short. The slogan is "where there is
pain there is gain". The method underneath it is more careful than the
slogan.

## The argument

He opens on the failure everyone recognises: you bought the breakout, got
stopped, then watched the market go where you said it would. If a level is
easy enough for his 80-year-old grandmother to draw, everyone has drawn it,
and its reliability is minimal. That is the retail-versus-institution
argument of [episode 4](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md) applied to a single tool.

The mechanism comes from [episode 1](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md): the market is
an auction and moves up until the last buyer buys. The distinction he
insists on is that resistance is not made by aggressive sellers. It is made
by buying drying up while passive sellers sit above. He shows four tests of
the same ES resistance near 5665 across five days; the tops read 29 volume
with delta 29, then 136 and 136, then 51 and 51: all aggressive buying,
nothing lifted. On the fourth test a delta he calls "plus 1200" goes into
the level and the market cannot break out; a passive seller is absorbing.
Then the mirror at support, where a delta of minus 451 into a double bottom
is absorbed by passive buyers. Two signatures, then: the volume tail, and
absorption.

Both have been arrived at independently on the Vorwald channel. What that
channel's [Market Profile video](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md) calls a buying or
selling tail, single prints at the extreme where the last participant
traded, is Carmine's volume tail read from TPO shape instead of delta. Its
[order-flow system video](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md) defines absorption as a
buyer repeatedly lifting a level with price failing to advance because a
large seller is absorbing, which is Carmine's fourth test. Neither cites the
other.

The one idea here that no other source in the corpus states is the weak
level. The same double bottom can be strong or weak depending on who caused
the bounce. At the first low a large seller was absorbed and a tail formed;
at the second the bounce came from aggressive buyers, people who will need
to sell when it fails, so he calls that support weak, and it breaks. The
Vorwald channel's [weak highs and lows](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md), extremes
built by weak hands that tend to get revisited, is the kindred idea reached from
profile shape rather than delta.

That makes the conflict with that channel's [podcast
episode](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md) worth stating exactly. There, the claim is
that support and resistance "do not truly exist as fixed price points".
Against an episode entirely about validating support and resistance, that is
a frontal contradiction. But Carmine narrows it himself, twice: "it doesn't
have to be a specific exact price, it could be a range of prices", and
later, "we can make it a zone". What he defends is a zone validated by flow,
not a line. The dispute is real on the headline, smaller on the mechanics,
and neither side brings statistics.

His three plays follow. First, let the obvious level break and enter on the
retest, never the initial break; that is the pullback-after-breakout entry
the Vorwald channel teaches in its [volume profile
video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) and contradicts in its own [scalping
video](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md), and Carmine repeats it in [episode
6](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md). Second, reversals off trapped participants, the
one he uses most. Third, get positioned before the level "with valid
confirmation" and use the level as the target. That phrase is the C of the
CLC rule two episodes before [episode 7](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md) names it:
context, then location, then confirmation read on the live tape, never from
a candle close. On that point he and the Vorwald channel part company, since
that channel's [PbD videos](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md) require a close, not a
wick.

On numbers the series is silent. Eight episodes never state a win rate, a
delta threshold or a sizing rule; the deltas here run from 29 to about 1200
and are all read the same way, because the size is not the signal, the
failure to move is. The Vorwald channel supplies what Carmine does not. Its
[heatmap video](../Vd83oo_geMk/CARD.md) puts a "truly large" resting ES
order above 250 to 300 contracts, resting size rather than executed delta,
but the only calibration on offer. Its volume profile video gives 200 to 300
trades before statistics mean anything and, in a worked example at a 50% hit
rate, an average risk-reward of "maybe a two", a hedge and not a floor.

The trade is a short journaled at $21,875 on 25 contracts, 17.5 points, with
a 3-point stop and a realised 5.83R. Two cautions. The stop and risk put the
entry at 5643, about 25 points below the 5668.50 double top he has
shown, so this was not a short taken at the top; the "6 to 1" belongs to an
entry made well into the move. And it is one of seven journaled trades
across the series, all winners, no loser shown anywhere. It illustrates the
method. It does not test it.

## In his words

**The question he starts from** *(00:00)*

> "How many times have you longed a breakout above a resistance level, or
> shorted a breakdown below a support level, for you to stop out of the
> market for a loss because it reversed on you as soon as it broke that
> support or resistance level?"

**The grandmother test** *(02:07)*

> "Now the way that I look at it is, if it's as easy as someone like my
> 80-year-old grandmother could go and pull up a chart and plot a normal high
> or low or support resistance level, then the reliability of it is going to
> be very minimal and the efficiency of it is going to be reduced, if
> everybody could do it, or if everybody sees it, or if it's the easiest
> thing that majority of other traders do."

**Why resistance forms** *(03:43)*

> "Now in terms of a market's auction, what actually causes resistance to
> form in the market is resistance of higher prices. If the market begins to
> rally and we start trending up, the market will eventually reverse and
> start moving lower once the last buyer is factored into the market. So in
> terms of an auction, the market will move up until the last buyer buys, and
> it's our objective to figure out when the last buyer will buy, because this
> is when a reversal will happen and this is when a resistance level will
> form that we can look to capitalize on."

*(04:21)*

> "A lot of traders believe that there is more aggressive selling which
> causes the market to drop, that validates a resistance level: every single
> time we come into the resistance the aggressive sellers are so strong that
> the market drops. But in terms of an auction, what actually causes the
> resistance level to form is when the buying dries up and the passive
> sellers are greater than all the aggressive buyers coming in."

> **Editor —** This is the load-bearing distinction of the episode:
> exhaustion, not aggression, makes a level. The Vorwald channel's
> definition of absorption, a buyer repeatedly lifting a level with price
> failing to advance because a large seller is absorbing, is the same
> mechanism reached separately.

**Support, and a range instead of a line** *(06:39)*

> "Same thing for support. What actually causes the support level to form?
> Well, the market's going to drop until the last seller sells. Once the last
> seller sells, the market will start moving up, and this is how we can look
> to capitalize on a bounce off of a level of support. So our job now is to
> look and observe, for the auction, for the last seller to sell. In this
> example we could see now a support level off of a range of prices instead
> of a singular price level. We have just this range of activity here, where
> we have multiple tests and multiple bounces off of this range … Call it
> maybe four passive buyers at $100,000 but only one aggressive seller
> hitting $100,000. The market has only one opportunity, or one way for it to
> go, and that's going to be to the upside."

> **Editor —** "A range of prices instead of a singular price level" is
> worth remembering when the Vorwald podcast says levels "do not truly exist
> as fixed price points". Carmine is not defending a fixed price point.

**Why the candle is not enough** *(08:05)*

> "Now to really validate this even better, we have to look at the order flow
> to understand a strong support or resistance level. We cannot rely
> entirely, strictly, off of a candlestick chart, because this does not show
> the true intention of who is buying or who is selling. We want to validate
> that if a resistance level is forming, this resistance level is forming
> because the buying volume has dried up."

**Test one: the volume tail** *(08:30)*

> "So in this example, point number one, we're going to see the market had
> aggressively sold off and we had a very large drop to the downside. If we
> were to look at the order flow off of this footprint chart, what we're
> going to see up here at 5665 is, number one, a volume tail, and on this
> volume tail we can see that there is 29 total volume and the 29 positive
> delta. That means at the high of day there was all aggressive buyers, yet
> the market could not move higher. The market moves up until the last buyer
> buys. This shows that the auction has finished at these prices, and all of
> this aggressive buying is near the highs. There's tons of buying entering
> the market, yet the market could not move up. In fact the market had sold
> off. This shows the sellers are very passive up here, and this is what's
> going to form the resistance level: when there's more passive orders than
> there are aggressive orders."

> **Editor —** The 29/29 cell is real; frames put it one tick above the
> line. What he calls a volume tail is what the Vorwald channel's Market
> Profile video calls a buying or selling tail: single prints at the extreme,
> where the last participant traded. Same observation, two vocabularies, no
> cross-citation.

**Test two: aggression absorbed** *(09:22)*

> "Point number two, this is a day later. A day later the market hits this
> resistance level, and again, it doesn't have to be a specific exact price,
> it could be a range of prices. … At the high, we're going to see 136 total
> volume and we're going to see a positive delta of 136. On top of that,
> we're going to see the market break this white line, which is where that
> original high was, or that original resistance level, and we're going to
> see a lot of blue positive delta on this footprint chart. This tells me
> that buyers were very aggressive, but the sellers were more passive than
> those aggressive buyers, and the buyers dried up, could not bring the
> market higher, and the market moved up until these buyers gave up."

**Test three** *(10:36)*

> "Point number three forms, the market comes up. Now in this example we
> could see the same thing: at the high we see 51 total volume, 51 positive
> delta. That's not as important as all the aggressive buying coming in at
> the level of resistance, and then all of the aggressive selling steps in.
> So now this is a little bit of a different example, because the aggressive
> selling steps in. … When we see a resistance level forming with a lot of
> aggressive buying, but the passive sellers are outweighing those aggressive
> buyers, this is going to validate a very strong resistance level."

> **Editor —** He flags this one as different, the test where sellers also
> turned aggressive rather than merely sitting passive. The card folds it in
> with the first two as the same signature; his own account is a shade more
> nuanced.

**Test four: the passive seller** *(11:28)*

> "The market rallies up into 5665, that's the same level that I mentioned
> earlier, all the aggressive buying, a plus 1200 delta, yet the market can't
> break out. There is a passive seller sitting here absorbing, and the buyers
> are becoming exhausted, and this is validating a very strong resistance
> level. And we can use this to our advantage, or use this information, to
> short this resistance level rather than looking to long this resistance
> level."

> **Editor —** Nothing on screen reads 1200; the frames show the largest
> cell as 1276 delta on 6940 volume, so "plus 1200" is his round-off. Note
> the range across the four tests: deltas of 29, 136, 51 and about 1200, all
> read identically. The size is never the signal, the failure to move is,
> and no episode in the series ever states a threshold. The nearest thing
> the corpus has is the Vorwald heatmap video's "truly large" resting ES

**The mirror at support** *(12:27)*

> "Here we have a volume tail [as] the market selling off, which forms the
> first, number one, low. And now number two, the low that forms here: we're
> going to see the double bottom is because of the aggressive selling here.
> You could see a negative delta of minus 451 and then another volume tail
> over here, indicating that the market moved down, the selling pressure was
> very strong, the market sold off until the selling gave up. The selling
> became exhausted, and then the passive buyers outweighed the selling, and
> then the market rallies up and forms that level of support."

**Not the literal last print** *(13:04)*

> "Remember, the concept is simple: the market will move down until the last
> seller sells. Now, you don't have to take that literal and look for
> literally the last sell bubble to come in, or the last sell print to come
> in. It's just simply, in terms to make it simple, until the selling dries
> up and until the passive buyers are greater than the aggressive sellers,
> then the market will move down, and once that occurs, that's when the
> support level will form."

*(13:47)*

> "And this is a 30 minute footprint here. When the market forms the low,
> the same exact thing, we're just seeing all the selling drill into the
> market. And if there's a lot of selling drilling into the market but the
> market is not moving down, we have somebody who is passively buying,
> passively absorbing, and the sellers, once they become exhausted and they
> stop selling, the market only has one option and that's to move up."

> **Editor —** Two things. "You don't have to take that literal" is the
> honest version of the slogan, and the version to keep. And "a 30 minute
> footprint" is stated here in plain words; the card describes the footprint
> in this episode as range bars and does not record this. Whether the
> support chart is a different configuration from the resistance chart is
> unresolved.

**What makes a level weak** *(14:27)*

> "Now let's talk about what makes a support or resistance level weak. Right
> here we could see a solid double bottom, and people would mark a simple
> support level and say hey, as soon as this level of support breaks I am
> going to take the market short. Now in some cases that could be great, and
> in a lot of cases that's going to be really bad approach to the market."

*(15:13)*

> "Number one over here, in real time, looks like a solid support level,
> because we see a really large negative delta, a very large seller, we also
> see a volume tail forming here. So at point number one this is a solid
> support level. However, point number one really isn't support until point
> number two forms. However, on point number two there's nothing significant
> here indicating that sellers became exhausted. In fact, there's a lot
> indicating here that aggressive buyers are strong. What makes a weak
> support level is if there's a lot of aggressive buyers trying to bring the
> market up higher. What makes a strong support level is if there's a lot of
> aggressive sellers that can't bring the market lower, because the market
> moves down until that last seller sells. In terms of support, if what
> causes the market to bounce and formulate a low, or formulate a support
> level, is aggressive buyers, then this becomes a level of support that is
> weak, that is likely to be broken at some other point in the future. We
> could see later in the session, about 10, 15 minutes after, the market
> breaks this double bottom and this old support level then becomes
> resistance."

> **Editor —** The sharpest idea in the episode, and the only one here that
> no other source in the corpus states in this form: the same bounce is
> strong or weak depending on who made it. A bounce made by buyers is made
> by people who will have to sell when it fails. The Vorwald channel's "weak
> highs and lows", extremes built by weak hands that tend to be revisited and
> broken, is the kindred idea, reached from profile shape rather than delta.

**Obvious and non-obvious** *(17:20)*
