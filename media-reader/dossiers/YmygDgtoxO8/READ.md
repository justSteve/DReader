# Read: The ONLY Volume-Profile-Video you will EVER need

> **Volume profile built from the ground up: why volume stacks where the market lingers, why the market comes back to those prices in about 70% of phases, and how yesterday's value area, an inside day and an overlap level each tell you what to look for today.**

- **Channel:** Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge · **Uploaded:** 2025-11-23 · **Duration:** 13:49
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YmygDgtoxO8

## In brief

The channel's primer on volume profile and the corpus's most-watched video,
and it earns that by explaining the tool instead of asserting it. The presenter
draws a profile by hand: a hundred trades at a price, then seventy more, so a
hundred and seventy stack at that level. Where the market lingers, volume piles
up; where it passed through quickly, there is a gap.

Then the part most explainers skip: why any of this should predict anything.
Large participants need time to build positions, so the prices where they built
them are the prices the market is drawn back to. He puts a number on it, about
70% of market phases, and gives the vocabulary that follows: point of control,
value area high and low, unfair price outside them, low-volume nodes the market
bounces off.

The second half applies it to yesterday's value area, an inside day and an
overlap level. No trade is taken and no entry, stop or target is given; this is
the map, not the route.

## The argument

He opens with a limit rather than a promise. The profile shows where a market
is in balance and where it is not, and it should not be traded in isolation;
what it gives you is the place where a breakout, a counter trade or a trend
continuation makes sense. The channel's companion volume-profile video takes
the same posture, that [a level tells you only that something could
happen](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md): this one builds the idea, that one supplies
the proof.

The construction is the best teaching in the video. The profile sums the trades
at each price, not under each bar: a hundred trades at a level, seventy more on
the return, a hundred and seventy in the profile. Where the market spends time it
also does business, so time and volume stack together; where it moved fast there
is little of either.

Then the mechanism, which is Carmine Rosato's auction premise. In balance everyone is satisfied; out of balance the big players are
not, because they need time to build positions, and so the market returns to
the fair prices where they did. Carmine's foundation episode argues [price moves
only when aggressive orders meet passive ones](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md) and that
balance is where the two sides agree; this presenter reaches the same place
from the participants' needs rather than from the tape. Elsewhere he gives the routing time behind it,
[one and a half to two days](../aursfDVYzUk/CARD.md).

The vocabulary follows. The point of control is the price with the highest
accumulated volume. Value area high and low are where the market starts to move
into imbalance, a sharper definition than "the middle 70%"; beyond them is
unfair price. The market returns to fair price in about 70% of market phases,
the same figure the channel gives for [how often the market sits in balanced
phases](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md), and its Market Profile videos put the value
area itself at [roughly 70% of volume or time](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md), refined
elsewhere to [68.2%, one standard deviation](../aursfDVYzUk/CARD.md). Consistent
across six videos; sourced in none. Low-volume nodes close the list: thin zones
the market bounces off.

One line is dropped and never picked up. Many traders treat the point of
control as the key reference for entries, "but we see it a bit differently."
He does not say how, here or anywhere in the sweep. The nearest candidate is the
channel's [time-weighted versus volume-weighted point of
control](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md), but that is our inference, not his sentence.

The applied half turns on acceptance. Yesterday's value area is drawn on today's
chart; the new day trades straight into it, so the probability is high that the
market has acceptance and wants fair prices again; when it fails to go back out,
the logical consequence is that it wants to trade the whole of yesterday's
range. That is the channel's [value-area re-entry rule](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md)
stated without the word rule, and the same prior-day value area the channel's
[one-minute scalping video](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md) builds its entire context on.

When the market falls out of value it often accelerates first, because
participants who bet on the range continuing are forced out below the level.
Note who is selling: trapped positions closing, not anyone hunting them. The channel's podcast agrees, [conceding the
mechanical stop-out and denying the agency](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md); Carmine's
premise episode reads [the same move as patient money deliberately buying the
bait](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md). Same print, opposite story about its author.

The support-and-resistance section is where the profile becomes a trade
location. An inside day means total balance against the day before. The next
day breaks lower, and in the afternoon the market tests the old balance from
below; since it has already accepted lower prices, that test is where he looks
for a counter trade back down. Carmine reaches the same location by a different
instrument: [fade the first test of a break when the aggressive volume gets no
follow-through](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md), and a level is [only real once the
order flow confirms it](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md). This presenter confirms with
acceptance on the profile rather than with the tape. Weaker proof, same trade.

The last idea is the overlap, where several days' value highs, volume nodes or
points of control share a price range. Returning into one after accepting much
lower prices, he says, makes an explosive breakout above it very likely. Then,
in the next breath, news pushes price up to that same level and is pushed back
because "everyone here will be in a bad position." The two halves do not obviously agree.

What the video never does is say which of its three trade types to take. That
choice is made elsewhere on the channel, where the VWAP video says to [classify
the structure first](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md) and the PbD videos give [balance
and the two trend phases their letters](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md). Read this one
as the vocabulary those videos assume.

## In his words

**Not in isolation** *(00:40)*

> "The volume profile is a very simple tool that can help us become much more
> aware of where markets are in balance or where they are out of balance. And
> it's very important for me to say upfront that you shouldn't use it in
> isolation for trading, but rather you can use it to identify zones and price
> areas where it makes sense to trade. Specifically, it makes sense to generate
> breakout trades, look for counter trades, or apply trend following strategies
> in the market to follow the global money."

> **Editor —** Three trade types named in one sentence and no rule for choosing
> among them, here or later. The channel does publish one, in its VWAP video:
> classify the market's structure first, then trend-follow in a trend and
> mean-revert in balance. This video assumes you will find that out elsewhere.

**How a profile is built** *(01:52)*

> "The volume profile clearly shows us exactly how much trading volume was
> transacted in this particular price movement with the total volume summed up
> for each of the respective price ranges. This means that unlike what you might
> be used to, we are not showing price ranges with their corresponding volumes
> underneath here. [...] For example, you can see that in this price range here,
> 100 trades were executed in my example. Now, the market has traded this price
> range again and let's say another 70 trades were executed there. This means
> that when summed up, we would have a total of 170 trades at this price level.
> So, what happens now with the volume profile? The volume profile adds up these
> respective trades at the corresponding price levels."

> **Editor —** The clearest statement in the corpus of what a profile is: volume
> summed at each price, across time, rather than under each bar. Everything else
> in the video is a consequence of this one addition.

**Time and volume stack together** *(03:07)*

> "Simply put, for example, if you see that a market has spent a lot of time at
> a certain price range, the probability is relatively high that a lot of trades
> have also taken place there. In price ranges like here, where very little
> trading has occurred, there is usually also very little volume traded at those
> corresponding price levels."

> **Editor —** Note "the probability is relatively high", not "always". The
> channel's Market Profile videos make a method out of the exceptions: the
> places where the most-time price and the most-volume price diverge are what
> they call the anomaly.

**Why the market comes back** *(04:05)*

> "It simply gives us the information that where the market is in balance,
> everyone is satisfied. And where the market is out of balance, there is often
> dissatisfaction among the big players. Why is that? Because large market
> participants need time to enter and build up their positions and that's why
> the market will always try to adapt to such fair price levels and return to
> them."

> **Editor —** This is the load-bearing claim of the whole tool and he states it
> as a mechanism, not a pattern: size needs time, so size leaves value where it
> worked. Carmine Rosato's auction premise reaches the same conclusion from the
> tape. Neither cites the other.

**The vocabulary** *(04:43)*

> "There is the so-called point of control. This is the point where the market
> has accumulated the highest total volume at a given price level. [...] Then
> there are the so-called value area high and low. These are the price areas
> where the market starts to move into an imbalance. This means if I transfer
> this over here, everything above the value area high and below the value area
> low is considered an unfair price area. This means that everywhere here, the
> market felt very comfortable. And in this price area and in this price area
> within the corresponding time frame, the market simply did not feel
> comfortable."

**The 70%** *(05:36)*

> "The market will typically attempt to return to fair price areas when it is
> being used by large market participants to build up or reduce positions
> accordingly. And this is the case in about 70% of market phases. That's just
> something you need to know. For us, this means we can look to see if we can
> find trades here or here in order to trade back into equilibrium. That's the
> first result we get from the relevant information."

> **Editor —** Read the first sentence whole. The return to fair price is tied
> to a condition, that large participants are using the area to build or reduce
> positions, and "typically" survives in the dub. The 70% is the same figure
> this channel gives in six videos for how often the market is in balance. It
> is consistent; it is never sourced.

**Low-volume nodes** *(06:07)*

> "Then in addition there are the so-called low volume areas, low volume nodes.
> There are many terms for this and these price areas are zones with very low
> volume for the respective time frame we're looking at. And the market often
> bounces off these areas. And the simple reason for this is that it likes to
> move down here, bounce off, and return to equilibrium. And that's basically
> all there is to it."

**On the chart, and the line he never finishes** *(06:37)*

> "Here you can see the price movement of a market up to the end of the day. A
> new day has started here. The corresponding volume profile is this one here.
> And so we can immediately see here the point of control which many consider to
> be an important reference price for entering trades. But we see it a bit
> differently. And here we have the value area high which is the price range
> where the market starts to move into unfair price regions above it. And here
> we have the value area low which is the price range where the market then
> moves below into unfair price areas."

> **Editor —** "We see it a bit differently" is the one thing in this video he
> announces and does not explain, and the sweep of twenty-one videos never
> returns to it. The card carries it as an open item.

**Yesterday's value, today** *(07:20)*

> "Let's transfer all of this to the next day and then we can see quite clearly
> what the market did on this day. [...] On this day, it started here and
> immediately moved into the previous day's balance area. So we know that the
> probability is high that the market now has acceptance and wants to trade fair
> prices again. Then it moved up here and showed us that no, it's not going back
> into unfair price areas. So the logical consequence is that it wants to trade
> the entire price range of the previous day. In other words, this value area
> from the previous day. We can only refer to that one because this one here is
> new."

> **Editor —** This is the channel's re-entry rule in practice: come back into
> the prior value area, find acceptance, and expect the market to traverse the
> whole profile. The Market Profile video states it as a rule with a target; here
> it is walked through on one day. The one-minute scalping video on this channel
> uses exactly this prior-day value area as its only context.

**When the market falls out of value** *(08:09)*

> "Now may be an interesting moment for you to see what happens when the market
> falls below such a level. Very often it accelerates at first. And why is that
> the case? Because a lot of market participants fall out of balance and then
> they sell their positions below that level because they had speculated that
> the market would continue to move back and forth."

> **Editor —** Notice who sells. Trapped range traders closing out, nobody
> hunting them. That is the position this channel's podcast takes, conceding
> that positions "have to be closed" while denying anyone engineers it, and it
> is the opposite of Carmine Rosato's premise episode, where the same move is
> patient money deliberately taking the other side. The print is identical;
> the story about who made it is the dispute.

**The footprint, in passing** *(08:36)*

> "This is a candlestick and this candlestick is now split into bid and ask
> prices. [...] What we see here are all the different prices that have been
> traded. And yes, this is a so-called footprint chart. I'll make a separate
> video about that later. But what I want to show you is that you can also sum
> up, for example, the volume profile for the corresponding price range. And you
> can immediately see where the market had its equilibrium. And this information
> helps us when we see that the market for example is moving further and further
> away from this price range because then we know oh everyone who is in
> equilibrium here could now run into trouble."

> **Editor —** The profile of a single candle is its footprint collapsed to one
> column. The point he draws from it is the same as above: distance from
> equilibrium is measured in trapped positions.

**Support and resistance, with volume** *(09:28)*

> "How does all of this help us to determine support and resistance? That is
> technical resistance and support zones which are combined with trading
> volumes. [...] What does this inside day tell us? Meaning this day that was
> completely within the previous day's range. It tells us very clearly that
> everything is in total balance compared to the previous day. But now we have
> the market opening on the next day. And now we see okay the market goes down
> and now changes its balance. And look what often happens in the afternoon. The
> market tests this imbalance area here again. And then since it has already
> indicated that it accepts lower prices and the major market participants now
> feel comfortable at lower levels, the logical consequence is that we can use
> such a price area here because we know that the market is in imbalance
> compared to previous days and weeks depending on which price area we are
> looking at. And here we can look for a counter trade in the direction of the
> trend or in the context direction. This means as we have understood when
> supply and demand shift there is a new acceptance of prices at lower levels
> and thus when the market tests an area the probability is high that it will
> move back there again and that is exactly the case here."

> **Editor —** A "counter trade in the direction of the trend" sounds like a
> contradiction and is not: the trade is counter to the afternoon retest and with
> the day's new direction. It is Carmine Rosato's fade of the first test of a
> break, and his insistence that a level is only real once the market confirms
> it, arrived at from the profile instead of the tape. The confirmation here is
> acceptance, which is slower and looser than live order flow. The location is
> the same.

**Overlaps** *(11:20)*

> "We can look at if each of these structures here represents a day then we can
> see where for example we have so-called overlaps, and overlaps are for example
> volume nodes, value highs or also several POCs within a price range. These are
> interesting overlap levels. And when the market on a given day moves back into
> such a price area like here where we clearly know the probability that the
> market will break out explosively above this level is very high. Why is that?
> Because here we have accepted significantly lower prices. The major market
> participants have shifted their range from above to below. Now for example
> some news comes out, the market pushes up here and then tells us all right,
> this is where it ends, because if we go above this level now everyone here
> will be in a bad position, and being in a bad position is generally not cool,
> so the market participants push the price back down."

> **Editor —** Two claims in one breath. First: returning into an overlap after
> accepting lower prices makes an explosive break above it very likely. Second:
> news pushes price up to that level and it is rejected because a break would
> leave everyone badly positioned. Either the dub has lost a clause or he means
> the level is where the decision is made and can go either way; the card
> records only the breakout half. Treat "explosive breakout" as one of two
> outcomes he describes at the same level, not a rule.

**Summing up** *(12:35)*

> "You can use the volume profile to locate areas of balance and imbalance.
> [...] You can then identify the appropriate setups for breakouts, trend
> continuations or counter trades. And always keep in mind whether prices are
> fair or unfair. [...] There are no secrets in the stock market. Keep it simple
> should always be the motto."
