## Terminology map — Arjo against the corpus

The terms Steve noticed "don't line up" are the ICT/SMC lexicon meeting the
order-flow lexicon. Same words, different referents:

| Arjo says | He means | Carmine / Vorwald (order flow proper) | SMDX / Trading Notes (same school) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **liquidity** | swing highs/lows (and prior-candle H/L) as places where stops and breakout orders *presumably* rest — inferred from price only | passive limit orders you can *see*: DOM depth, Bookmap bands; Carmine: "retail is the liquidity (bait fish)"; Vorwald: the book is the market | resting stops + stop-entry breakout orders; pools above equal highs / below equal lows (Trading Notes 01:05–02:16) |
| **sweep** | trade through a swing point, reject, reverse toward the opposite swing point | Carmine *trapped participants / absorption*: aggressive volume with no follow-through, read on the footprint; Vorwald: stop-hunting *"a myth"*, single-digit contracts print below lows | SMDX: "1–2 candle snap-back = liquidity sweep, not ChoCH"; Trading Notes step 2 "wait for the sweep" |
| **run on liquidity** | trade through and continue to the next same-side swing point | Carmine breakout continuation: confirm volume, pullbacks hold; *"never buy the initial break, buy the pullback"* | SMDX **BOS**: close above the most recent swing high |
| **two sting** (1st sting → retracement → 2nd sting) | break, pullback, re-break; wicks count | Carmine step 2: a defended retest proves participants "even more powerful" | SMDX BOS + retest entry (v10) — but SMDX requires a **close** ("wick above ✖"); Arjo accepts a wick |
| **sweep confirmed when the FVG/OB fails** | after the reject, price makes a lower low below the minor low formed at the FVG/OB | — (no equivalent; Carmine reads the reversal in the footprint at the level) | SMDX **ChoCH**: close below the most recent HL, confirmed by internal LH+LL; Trading Notes step 3: drop TF, wait for ChoCH |
| **order block** | the whole retracement (the down-candles) before the leg that broke the swing point | — | SMDX: the single candle right before a swing point — narrower |
| **fair value gap** | three-candle gap | — | SMDX: "void where no resting limit orders" |
| **PD arrays** | the set {swing H/L, FVG, OB} | — | ICT term; SMDX uses the parts without the label |
| **trend** | running highs = bullish; running lows = bearish; sweeping both = consolidation | auction / balance-imbalance, profile shape (Vorwald PbD) | market structure HH/HL, BOS/ChoCH — the same content |
| **institutional story** | breakout traders (large, institutional) get "suffocated" on a sweep | Carmine: retail buys breakouts, institutions *sell into* them; Vorwald: no one is hunting | SMDX "YOUR BREAKOUT ENTRY = THEIR EXIT LIQUIDITY"; Trading Notes "NOBODY IS HUNTING YOUR STOPS" |

Two mappings are worth stating outright:

- **Arjo's sweep/run confirmation *is* SMDX's ChoCH/BOS on a lower
  timeframe**, with looser rules (wicks count, "some rejection" suffices).
  Nothing in the video is new relative to the SMDX series; what is new is the
  packaging — the target table, the two-sting name, the red/green zone — and
  the insistence on marking both outcomes before choosing.
- **His one unusual claim inverts the corpus.** Everyone else casts breakout
  buyers as *retail* and the fuel; Arjo says they are *"large institutions
  and hedge funds"* (38:35) with *"real, large orders"*. No evidence is
  offered. Vorwald's footprint observation — single-digit contracts printing
  through chart lows — is direct evidence against "large orders rest at swing
  points". Arjo half-concedes the point himself at 40:06–40:30: most of his
  audience trades prop-firm demo accounts whose orders *"never go to the real
  market"*, so their liquidity *"is not even relevant"*.

## Credibility assessment

**What is credible.**

- **Definitions are explicit and consistently applied.** He works to make
  sweep/run objective (09:30 *"We can define this objectively"*), gives an
  order of operations (34:34) and — rare in the genre — a **do-nothing
  zone** (35:34). The Trading Notes card found no entry rule; Arjo at least
  tells you where *not* to act.
- **He hedges honestly and claims nothing he cannot show.** *"very likely"*,
  *"often times"*, *"won't always happen"*, *"this is how I interpret it"*,
  *"if this doesn't make any sense to you, perfect. Just let it sit there"*
  (45:10). No win rate, no P&L, no *"I made X"*. Nothing is fabricated
  because nothing quantitative is asserted.
- **The mechanics he does state are right.** The order-matching table
  (43:12) is correct. The trend section reduces to Dow-theory market
  structure and he says so (49:13).
- **The charts are real and current.** The ES bar matches Strader's tape to
  the tick; the session clock is live. The NQ 15m examples are replay, which
  is normal for teaching.
- **Low-pressure funnel.** Two pitches in 54 minutes, both brief, one for a
  free Discord. Mentorship is sold via description, not on air.

**What is not.**

- **"Confirmation" is after-the-fact relabelling.** A sweep is confirmed
  once price has already made a lower low below the retracement low; a run
  is confirmed once price has already re-broken the high. Both are structure
  breaks that have *happened*. The framework then names the next swing point
  as the target — which in a trending market is close to tautology. He
  offers no hit rate for "target reached before the opposite swing point",
  and the video contains no number that could be checked.
- **The rules are discretionary at every edge.** Which swing counts (he
  says all are fractal — so any); whether a wick is a sting; how much
  rejection is *"some rejection"* (35:00); which timeframe to drop to
  (18:12–19:38 gives three options). Two traders would not mark the same
  chart the same way. Same gap as Trading Notes (`IUWvHVout94`).
- **The "institutional story" is asserted, not evidenced, and partly
  self-contradicted** (above). *"Orders need to be matched at all times"*
  (39:43) conflates resting orders with executed trades — a resting stop
  needs no counterparty until it triggers. The reasoning that sellers *need*
  the breakout buyers' stops (42:40) attributes intent to aggregate order
  groups; it is the ICT agency narrative in mild form, and it is exactly what
  Vorwald rejects on footprint evidence.
- **Causal language for chart features.** *"The swing high tries to push
  price lower … the FVG + OB might push price higher"* (22:21–23:15) treats
  drawn zones as forces. The consolidation explanation — stuck because both
  are holding — is circular: the zones are defined as places price reversed.
- **No credential.** 280K subscribers and "ex-prop firm owners" (a Discord
  blurb, not a claim about himself). Unknown name, unknown trading history.

**Verdict.** Credible as a **teacher of a coherent discretionary
market-structure framework**, honest about its limits, and free of the
fabrications that mark the worse end of this genre. **Not a source of facts
about order flow** — it measures nothing, and its one mechanism claim is
unsupported and cuts against the corpus's footprint evidence. The unusual
depth Steve heard is real, but it is depth of *systematisation of ICT
concepts*, not depth of order-book knowledge. Read it as the clearest
statement of the sweep-then-ChoCH architecture in the corpus, alongside
SMDX v9/v10 and Trading Notes.
