# Read: The Ultimate Multi-Timeframe Strategy for Perfect Entries (Every Time)

> **Volume exhaustion, the channel's one order-flow trigger for a reversal: find the level on the weekly and daily, and trade against it only when price trades back below where the volume swelled. A four-timeframe cascade with not a single number in it.**

- **Channel:** Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge · **Uploaded:** 2026-08-12 · **Duration:** 12:20
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HySZZSjMxF8

## In brief

He is arguing that most losing trades are taken on the wrong chart: a setup
applied on some random timeframe without knowing which trend is prevailing
above it. His remedy is a strict top-down walk. The weekly fixes the range,
the daily names the phase, the hourly reads structure and bias, and the
fifteen-minute chart exists to define risk. The higher timeframes supply the
level and the direction; the lowest supplies the stop.

What earns the video its place on the order-flow axis is the last two minutes.
At a level pre-located from above, "when a reversal occurs, the volume also
increases", and the trade is on only when price trades back down below where
that increased volume began. If it does not, "then I have to wait." That is
absorption, read off a plain volume histogram rather than delta, and it is the
second independent arrival at the mechanism in this corpus. It also shows the
presenter teaching the reversion trade, which his one-minute scalping video
appeared to contradict.

## The argument

The premise is that significance scales with timeframe: "the larger a time
period is, the more likely it is that the signal will be significant when a
technical level is broken." So locate the important areas on a high
timeframe, then expect "something bigger" at them on a smaller one. Smart Money
Decode X makes the same case in [its episode on switching
timeframes](../EW_TtV_rgzY/CARD.md), starting one notch lower at the daily,
with the same division of labour: the higher chart for direction, the lower for
timing.

The weekly gives him the last significant high and low, which define the
trading range. The daily answers a different question, which phase the market
is in. He reads a bullish environment, an impulsive push above prior highs,
then a second attempt at those highs with "absolutely no further trend
continuation", and concludes the market is in a large sideways phase whose
lower edge is now the likelier test. In passing he calls closing prices
"very, very important" and declines to say why: "I don't want to make it too
complicated now." The rule he means is stated elsewhere on the channel: closes
and opens confirm acceptance in [the beginners' PbD
video](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md), closes outside value confirm a move out of
balance in [the detailed PbD explainer](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md), and Smart
Money Decode X pins it to a close below the most recent higher low in [its
ChoCH episode](../iPDi9_nzn-o/CARD.md).

The hourly is where he reads structure, and he is careful to say that lower
highs and lower lows are "how someone who does the whole thing technically
would read it." His own interest is in how the move was made: the sell-off "has
taken place dynamically", faster down than up. He draws a line above which "the
trend structure is disturbed for now", but disturbed is not reversed: above it
he waits for lower lows to resume, still focused on short.

On the fifteen-minute chart the whole exercise resolves into a stop. He wants a
short "if the market was trading above this level and then runs back in",
confirmed by "a clear reversal signal" at the pre-located point, abandoned
above a defined price, exiting at the prior lows carried down from the hourly.
This is the idea-and-proof pair from [his volume-profile
video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) rebuilt across timeframes, and Carmine Rosato's
[context, location, confirmation](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md) in different words,
except that Carmine takes confirmation from the live tape and this presenter
does not say where his comes from until the volume section.

Option two is the more interesting trade. The market stops him out, pushes
above the level, "fails to hold and accepts the lower prices again", and that
"gives me a really nice B structure setup." The letter is PbD vocabulary the
card does not note: in [the crisis-PbD video](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md) the b is
"the aggressive trend phase downwards", and [the detailed PbD
explainer](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md) makes a breakout from a P or b the moment to
trade. In Carmine's language it is the trade of [his volume
episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md): never buy the initial break, buy its failure.
That matters because [the one-minute scalping card](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md) had
this presenter entering on the break. He teaches both postures and never says
which applies when; the nearest rule is in his VWAP videos
([here](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md) and [here](../VumVuGnCcFM/CARD.md)): classify
structure first, trend gets a pullback entry, balance gets a reversion target.

Then the contribution. At the pre-located high, "usually, when a reversal
occurs, the volume also increases", and "when it then trades back down from
that increased volume where it started, I've had exhaustion at the top."
Otherwise he waits. That is what Carmine calls absorption: aggressive volume at
an extreme with no follow-through, so a passive counter-party must be there.
Carmine reads it from [the footprint's delta](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md) and knows
which side was absorbed; this presenter reads [the order
book](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md) in other videos, but here has only a total-volume
histogram, a tall bar with no direction. Price trading back below where
the volume began is his proxy for what delta shows directly. It is also
Carmine's [test for a real level](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md): support or
resistance exists only once aggression exhausts into passive size there.

What is missing is everything measurable. No definition of "increased" volume,
no multiple, no bar count. No word on whether price must close below the volume
or merely trade below it; he says "trade", the card reads that as a
trade-through rule, but he never contrasts it with a close. No stop distance,
no target, a "very high risk reward ratio backed by logic and reason" never
given as a number. His closing point, that markets spend "a lot, a lot of time"
in sideways phases while large participants position, is the roughly 70% of
[his market-profile video](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md). The demonstration chart is
a different index future; the method transfers to the S&P, the levels do not.

## In his words

**Why top down** *(00:44)*

> "Multi-time frame simply means looking at different time units and ideally
> thinking from the top down if you're a day trader. Because the larger a
> time period is, the more likely it is that the signal will be significant
> when a technical level is broken, overtraded, or whatever. That's why we
> traders who use multi-time frame thinking always like to look from the top
> down. This means [...] if we locate important price areas in a very
> high-level time frame, then as day traders, we can expect something bigger
> in a smaller time horizon."

> **Editor —** The whole video hangs on this one sentence, and it is an
> assertion rather than an argument. Nothing later tests it. Smart Money
> Decode X builds the same top-down architecture and at least adds minimums
> for how many swings qualify a structure on each timeframe.

**Fixing the range on the weekly** *(01:34)*

> "Let's take [...] an example first. [...] Where was the last high? It was
> right here. Where was the last low of the movement that we currently find
> interesting? As you can clearly see, right here was the last significant
> low. This means that with this high and this low, I'm going to define the
> current trading range, and then I'll switch over to the daily chart."

> **Editor —** The demonstration runs on a Nasdaq futures chart and the price
> level he names is omitted here; the reader is S&P only, and the method does
> not depend on the instrument. Note what is absent even so: no rule for which
> high and which low count. "The movement that we currently find interesting"
> is the whole criterion.

**Classifying the daily** *(02:05)*

> "So now I'm going from the weekly candlestick chart to the daily chart, and
> I can already see, okay, right in the middle, that's not surprising, but
> what kind of market phase are we in right now? And you can clearly see that
> the market is currently moving sideways. For example, I can immediately
> locate and classify for myself here that we are in a very bullish
> environment. Why? We have successfully pushed above the previous highs
> here, and we have made a very clear impulsive move to the upside. [...] We
> did briefly manage to trade above those highs once again, but
> unfortunately, there was absolutely no further trend continuation to speak
> of. So, taking a step back, I am now looking down at the chart and asking
> myself, where exactly is the lowest point that is truly crucial for me to
> watch right now? [...] And so I have a large sideways phase here with
> further information that I'm now transferring to the next smaller time
> frame. The market phase rejection here, it didn't go any further, and so
> the probability is higher for now that the market will test the downside of
> this sideways phase."

> **Editor —** Bullish environment and sideways phase are both true at once in
> his reading: the impulse established the range, the failed retest of its
> top makes the bottom the likelier test. That second attempt with no
> continuation is, in Carmine Rosato's vocabulary, aggressive buying that got
> no follow-through, and Carmine would fade it. Here it only sets a bias.

**Closing prices, flagged and dropped** *(03:37)*

> "Because, and this is very, very important, always keep an eye on the
> closing prices. That gives you even more information. But we're looking at
> multi-time frame, I don't want to make it too complicated now."

> **Editor —** The video's description advertises deriving trend "cleanly
> using closing prices", and this is the entire treatment. The rule exists on
> the channel, stated in the PbD videos as closes and opens confirming
> acceptance rather than wicks. Whoever watches only this one leaves without
> it.

**The hourly: structure, and how the move was made** *(03:53)*

> "The probability is currently higher for me that the market could move
> downwards. Why? Because I could see the information in the daily chart that
> there is rejection. [...] But is that enough to locate a trade for me? Of
> course not. [...] Let's take some very simple strategic approach and say we
> have the last highest high, we have lower and lower highs. A next lower high
> could come somewhere here now. What else do we have? Lower lows. That's at
> least how someone who does the whole thing technically would read it. And
> for me, of course, it's much more exciting. What is the structure of the
> whole thing? And then I'll draw in the price course since we've been
> falling downwards. And I can already see that it's always been more dynamic
> on the way down. That gives me another clue. Yes, the sell-off has taken
> place dynamically so far."

> **Editor —** The distinction he draws is worth keeping: lower highs and
> lower lows are the technician's read, and he treats them as the weaker
> evidence. The stronger one is the character of the move, fast down and slow
> up. That is an auction observation, aggression on one side only, though he
> has no tool on screen to measure it and relies on the eye.

**Two things can happen** *(05:21)*

> "If we go above this, I have to be careful because then we no longer have
> lower highs. [...] And so I now have a very clear price range here where I
> say, 'If we go above this, the trend structure is disturbed for now.' But
> that doesn't mean that we're above this yet. Because now two things can
> happen. [...] If the market goes above this accordingly, I have a greater
> possibility in terms of risk that the market will run out here at the top
> again. But I'm actually still short oriented from the multi timeframe
> approach. And that means I would wait, for example, if it goes above here
> to see if it runs down again and makes lower lows for me again, and I still
> have the focus on short. That's one option."

> **Editor —** Disturbed, not reversed. The higher-timeframe bias survives a
> break of the hourly structure, and what he waits for is lower lows to
> resume. That is the difference between an invalidation line and a stop:
> this line changes his posture, not his position. He never says what would
> make him flip long.

**Carrying the levels down** *(06:40)*

> "If I see now, for example, that the market is forming a nice reversal here,
> then I know that there's a good chance that the market will move downwards
> from that point. [...] So, I'm going to go ahead and transfer these specific
> price ranges over to the chart, and then I'll carefully observe where all
> of this might actually take place. [...] And then I'll look to the left and
> see here. Here were the last lows. Here was the last range. The market
> could stop here for now. I will go ahead and transfer that information
> over, and by doing so, I'll suddenly have a much smaller, more manageable
> overview to work with in significantly shorter time periods than I was
> dealing with before."

**The fifteen-minute: defining risk** *(07:52)*

> "This is the hourly chart now and now I'll do the following and say I want
> to trade this on the 15-minute chart to define my risk well. And I know now
> what I'm looking for and where. That means I'm looking here, for example,
> for the possibility of a short position if the market [...] was trading
> above this level and then runs back in. I'm specifically looking for a
> clear reversal signal here right when the market reaches this critical
> point as that would confirm my setup for a potential entry. [...] I'm
> looking for a short trade here and I know that above this point it should
> be abandoned for now. And so by doing this, I can build a really great risk
> reward ratio for myself. I can confidently say, 'Look, this is the specific
> chance that I've already predefined and this is exactly what my risk is
> going to be on this trade.' And besides that, I also know that the market
> structure from the largest time frame is currently short. Because of this,
> I feel that I have a really big advantage in this trade. I have a very high
> risk reward ratio backed by logic and reason."

> **Editor —** The only reason given for the step to fifteen minutes is "to
> define my risk well", and that is the honest one: the lower timeframe buys a
> tighter stop, not a better signal. The "very high risk reward ratio" is
> never a number. Stop and target are drawn as zones on the chart and no
> price or point distance is spoken anywhere in the video.

**Option two: the B structure** *(09:18)*

> "Option two, the market stops me out right here, then it pushes above this
> level, but eventually it fails to hold and accepts the lower prices again.
> This gives me a really nice B structure setup to trade, for example. Now
> that I can see the trend continuation is still very much in play, I can
> confidently say that my next stop loss is positioned just above this level.
> Right here, where the market previously set that swing high. Here is my
> entry area somewhere, and down here is my plan for the exit again. So, it's
> also a good trade in terms of the risk-reward ratio."

> **Editor —** "B structure" is the b of PbD, the aggressive trend phase
> downward, forming here at the fifteen-minute scale after a failed break
> above resistance. The card records this as a failed-breakout re-entry and
> does not note the letter. Read as PbD, it links this video to the channel's
> flagship framework; read in Carmine Rosato's terms, it is the failed
> breakout he prefers to the break itself. Either way it is a reversion
> trade, from the presenter whose one-minute scalping video enters on the
> break. The accepted-back-inside condition is stated; how long acceptance
> must last is not.

**Volume exhaustion** *(10:17)*

> "I can now take a closer look at exactly how the trading volumes behave
> when the market forms these specific high points that I have already
> pre-located accordingly on the chart. This really helps me see the full
> picture. And usually, when a reversal occurs, the volume also increases.
> Either here or here. And when it then trades back down from that increased
> volume where it started, I've had exhaustion at the top. But if it doesn't
> trade below this volume here, for example, then I have to wait. And that's
> a very simple way and a very simple methodology to adapt this approach for
> yourself."

> **Editor —** This is the video. The sequence is: level found from above,
> price arrives, volume swells, price trades back below where the swell
> began, then and only then the counter-trade. It is Carmine Rosato's
> absorption arrived at independently, and neither cites the other. The cost
> of his version is that a volume histogram has no direction in it: he can see
> that a great deal traded and that price failed, not which side was
> absorbed. Carmine's delta shows that directly. Note also what is not said:
> no threshold for "increases", and no word on whether a close below is
> required or a trade-through suffices. He says "trade", and that is all.

**What multi-timeframe is for** *(11:17)*

> "Multi-timeframe is very useful for doing the following things. First, to
> locate where am I in the overall market environment? So, what can I expect?
> Second, have there already been breakouts from this large timeframe where I
> see that rejection was present? That gives me more confidence that the
> market will initially move back and forth in a sideways phase. And third, I
> can then very easily locate for myself in the small timeframe where the
> market is more likely to go because the big market participants use the big
> games for themselves as the market always needs a lot, a lot of time. Market
> participants like to use this time to place their respective capital in the
> markets during such large sideways phases."

> **Editor —** The dub garbles "the big games"; the sense of the sentence is
> that large participants use the long sideways phases to build positions,
> which is the channel's standing claim that markets sit in balance around 70%
> of the time. The second point is the sharpest thing in the summary: a
> breakout from the higher timeframe that was rejected raises his confidence
> in range, not in trend. That is the failed-breakout logic of option two
> promoted to the daily chart.
