# Read: PbD: The World's BEST trading strategy (explained in detail)

> **The fullest statement of the channel's PbD framework: P, b and D are the shapes a profile draws, a breakout from a P or b is the moment to trade, and a candle close is what confirms it.**

- **Channel:** Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge · **Uploaded:** 2026-01-23 · **Duration:** 12:08
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jXR1afMy-3o

## In brief

PbD is not an acronym, and this video never expands it; seven videos on this
channel use the term and none explains it. The letters are pictures. In a
Market Profile, an aggressive rally that then settles near its highs draws a
capital P: thin stem, fat top. A sell-off that settles at its lows draws a lowercase b.
A market that simply agrees on price draws a D, the bell. P and b are
imbalance, the roughly 30% of the time a market accelerates; D is balance, the
other 70%.

The two profiles answer two questions. Market Profile says who is dominating,
because it records time at price. Volume Profile says where the break points
are, because thin volume is where price slips out of fair value. One rule ties
them together: acceptance is read from where candles close, never from where
they wick. A breakout from a P or a b, confirmed by closes, is the moment to
trade.

## The argument

He opens with where the logic came from: Market Profile and Volume Profile,
because between them they answer two questions, who is dominating the market
and where the breaking points are. Then, he says, he analysed thousands of
patterns and asked whether all of it could be captured technically. The result
is a framework he claims to have published in Traders' Magazine years ago.
Neither is evidenced in the video.

The two-profile reading is the order-flow core. The grey blocks are the Market
Profile: time at price. A selling tail, single letters at the top, says
aggressive sellers were there and nobody stayed. Single prints say traders were
forced to buy, and the reasons he gives are obligations: hedging, some
fundamental reason to be in the market. The histogram beside it is the Volume
Profile: where the market found acceptance and, in the low-volume nodes, where
it slips into imbalance. Fair price is the value area, where large participants
can find counterparties for positions that take many hours to build. Everything
outside is unfair. This is the auction premise Carmine Rosato opens his series
with ([QaNPAaEnB5E](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md)) and the one Smart Money Decode X
builds its structure on; none of the three cites the others.

The regime split follows. P and b formations are the roughly 30% of the time
when a market accelerates. The remaining 70% is balance, where you trade value:
short at the top, long at the bottom. He anticipates the objection that a trend
strategy is useless most of the time and answers it flatly: no, because the D
is a setup too. The 70/30 figure appears on this channel at least five times,
in [K8qtT2_axPo](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md),
[YmygDgtoxO8](../YmygDgtoxO8/CARD.md), [Ly62G168MkQ](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md),
[43JaKHRvxHk](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md) and here, always the same round number,
never with a source. Consistent, which is worth something; unsupported, which
is worth remembering.

Then the rule the rest of the channel only gestures at. His thesis is that
where a market leaves balance, above or below the value area, he very often
sees candles opening and closing out there; so he uses closing prices to locate
the break-off edges in the profile. Closes define the fair range; whatever
trades beyond them is unfair price. He adds that you should not strip the wicks
off the chart: the extremes have their justification, they just decide nothing.
When the market breaks out of a balance, it is new closes forming beyond it,
with volume underneath, that make continuation likely. Whether continuation
holds is settled by what he calls the rules for counting closing prices. The
on-screen version is one German sentence: a breakout from a P-formation tells
you the decisive moment when you should trade.

That puts him on one side of a real divide. Carmine's CLC rule reads
confirmation on the live tape and refuses to wait for a candle close; his ep. 7
([7facFfjQ0UE](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md)) shows why, a stop that grows from about
two points to about seven while you wait for the bar. The Smart Money Decode X
series takes the other side, defining a change of character as a close below
the most recent higher low and a wick as no break at all
([dAlP8UZXT48](../dAlP8UZXT48/CARD.md)). This channel is with SMDX. It says so
here three times and again in [Ly62G168MkQ](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md) and
[uFxYcpiaOpw](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md), which closes the gap logged against
[HySZZSjMxF8](../HySZZSjMxF8/CARD.md), where the description promised a
closing-price rule and the video delivered an aside.

The scenarios are drawn in Paint. A setup below the range that pokes out and
comes back in will probably go back up. One that breaks out upward will
probably keep going. When the market drops, forms a P and buying resumes, he
calls that a break-in. The corpus had no name for the case: it is the failed
breakout seen from inside the range, the event Carmine fades on the first test
and [HySZZSjMxF8](../HySZZSjMxF8/CARD.md) enters on re-acceptance. If instead
the market falls back into the range and keeps going, the P has become a
reversal, and a close confirming the downside gives him the one target in the
video: below the start of the impulse. When the closes stop extending and you
are stopped out once or twice, the market is building balance; switch to
trading its edges. The VWAP video, [43JaKHRvxHk](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md), gives
the same regime rule from the other direction.

What he does not give matters as much. The counting rules are invoked three
times and never stated: how many closes, on what timeframe, how far beyond the
edge. There is no entry, stop, risk-reward, hit rate or trade. The title calls
it the world's best strategy and the channel calls its traders world champions;
every leaderboard the channel shows places them second or third, and the claim
stays unverified. What this video is, honestly, is the clearest map of the
framework. The strategy is still somewhere else.

> wann du traden solltest."

> **Editor —** A breakout from a P-formation tells you the decisive moment when
> you should trade. This is a caption on the Paint canvas, not speech; the
> slides on this channel stay in German even when the audio is dubbed. It is
> the whole entry model in one line, and the rest of the video is the
> reasoning behind it.

**Where it came from** *(00:43)*

> "About the logic I developed mainly to have something in the markets that
> makes sense to me, especially over multiple time frames. And how did I come
> up with it? I simply delved very deeply into market profile and volume
> profile, where on the one hand you can determine who is currently dominating
> the market and on the other hand you can see where the breaking points are."

> "I then analyzed thousands of patterns and setups for myself back then and
> observed: isn't there some way to capture all of this technically? And I
> found these possibilities and I want to explain them to you because I can
> tell you it's very simple. You just need to know one thing and that's exactly
> what almost no one does."

**The regime** *(01:27)*

> "This logic applies to round about 30% of markets. The P's and the b's are
> both structures, setups that typically cause a market to accelerate. The
> other 70% in the so-called balance phases, sideways markets, those are the
> market phases where you can trade value, short at the top, long at the
> bottom."

> **Editor —** The fifth time this channel gives the split, and the figure
> never moves. He adds that he published and demonstrated the logic in
> Traders' Magazine "I don't even know how many years ago"; nothing in the
> video evidences that, so it stays a claim. He also never says what P, b and
> D are. The key is in the crisis video,
> [m3IMdc7QwN4](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md): the letters are the shapes a Market
> Profile draws. P is an upward impulse that balanced near its highs, b is the
> mirror, D is the bell.

**Two profiles, two questions** *(02:09)*

> "What you see here, that's the volume profile and the gray areas you see in
> these wonderful blocks, that's the corresponding market profile. That means
> we layer them on top of each other. And with that we have two possibilities.
> On the one hand, we have the option to see who is currently dominating the
> market. For example, here with such a selling tail, aggressive sellers. How
> much time has the market spent at certain price levels? With this we can
> identify who is currently dominating the game, who is active in the market
> right now."

> "The second thing we can identify is through the volume profile. Where are
> the price areas where the market has so-called low volume nodes? In other
> words, zones where the markets slip into imbalance. Because as I already
> explained in the volume profile, we always have to consider where is the
> market unfair and where is the market fair priced."

> **Editor —** The cleanest division of labour between the two profiles
> anywhere in the corpus. Time at price answers *who*; volume at price answers
> *where*. Carmine reads the same two things from the footprint and the DOM as
> they print; this channel reads them from the day's distribution after the
> fact. Same questions, different clock.

**Not only a trend strategy** *(03:21)*

> "It is very important for you to understand that financial markets typically
> operate in these normal market phases approximately 70% of the total time.
> And now the question may arise, what do I want with a strategy that only
> operates in trending markets? And I have to say very clearly, no."

**What a P means** *(03:54)*

> "For example, it might look like this. The market comes down, then moves
> steadily upward and it forms a balance. What does that mean? That essentially
> means for us as traders, we can clearly identify that very strong buyers have
> come in right here. We can easily identify that the big institutional money
> feels quite comfortable at this level. And that is exactly why we need to go
> and analyze such market fragments very smartly for ourselves in the future.
> And I do this analysis using my closing prices."

> **Editor —** He is describing a P without naming it: impulse up, then
> balance near the top. The reading is order-flow reasoning, not
> pattern-matching. The impulse says aggressive buyers arrived; the balance
> above it says the size that arrived is content to hold there. Both halves are
> needed, and a rally that does not balance is not a P.

**Fair price** *(04:40)*

> "I also need to know that the value area is the price range where the market
> finds acceptance, where the big market participants simply feel comfortable
> because they can find buyers there for their trades, for the large positions
> they take, which can sometimes take many hours."

**The thesis** *(04:59)*

> "In the next step I went ahead and formulated a very simple thesis and said,
> always there, where the market moves out of balance, that is above the value
> area and below the value area, I very often see candlesticks with closing
> prices and opening prices. That might not sound easy at first, but I'm
> telling you it is easy."

> "So, what is the essence? The essence is that I use closing prices to locate
> where the so-called break-off edges are in the volume profile."

> **Editor —** This is the rule. Note how it is stated: as an observation ("I
> very often see") that hardened into a procedure ("I use closing prices to
> locate"). Carmine's ep. 7 argues the opposite, that waiting for a close tells
> you nothing about strength and only widens the stop. SMDX defines its change
> of character as a close below the last higher low. Of the three sources in
> this corpus that address the question, two read closes; the one that trades
> fastest does not.

**Single prints** *(05:46)*

> "In the next step I need to see who is currently dominating the market. And
> that brings us back to the market profile. In the market profile I can see,
> for example, if a market has single prints like here, then I know that
> traders were forced to buy very aggressively at this point. In this case, up
> here is the closing price and the market was pushed upwards. Down here was
> the balance area. This means that this price range was initiated by
> aggressive traders because, for example, they had to make hedging
> transactions or because they had some other fundamental reason for entering
> the market. And when I see something like this and identify it for myself,
> then it just makes perfect sense. And of course, up here the market is out of
> balance."

> **Editor —** "Forced to buy" is the important phrase. He reads single prints
> as obligation, not conviction: hedging, or a fundamental reason. It is the
> same account of institutional flow he gives in
> [NkQeOVDTAec](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md), where large participants must route
> orders whatever their view, and it is the reason Carmine gives for why
> aggressive volume is worth reading at all.

**A new equilibrium** *(06:33)*

> "Next comes the zone, the phase where the market starts to create balance
> again. A new equilibrium. This means I have the information that aggressive
> traders pushed the market up and now they're back in a fair price range.
> Aggressive traders push the market up and then, through the closing price
> count, have the opportunity to narrow down the fair price range again."

**Closes define, wicks stay** *(07:15)*

> "We simply determine this using closing prices. And these closing prices can
> be easily defined saying that everything above and below them are unfair
> prices. I'm not interested in those at all. Be mindful, don't go and use some
> methods to remove the candlesticks now. No, they're good because even the
> extremes always have their justification for existing."

> **Editor —** A more careful position than "closes confirm, wicks don't". The
> wicks stay on the chart because the extremes are real information about
> where price was rejected; they simply do not define the fair range. SMDX's
> slides put it more bluntly ("wick above, not a break"), but the two are
> saying the same thing.

**The breakout and the demand for evidence** *(07:46)*

> "At some point the market will break out of this zone again. Probably to the
> upside because the trend is aggressively upward, but there's just as much of
> a chance that we go downward. And now we absolutely require substantial
> evidence and confirmation here or up here, that the market will continue to
> move in an upward trajectory or a downward trend."

> "It makes sense to watch for new closing prices forming up here along with
> the corresponding trading volumes down here. This technical setup indicates
> that the likelihood that the prevailing market trend will persist is
> exceptionally high, suggesting a strong probability of continued price
> movement in that same direction. There is an interest in pushing the market
> back up here."

> **Editor —** "Probably to the upside, but just as much of a chance that we go
> downward" is a tension he does not remark on, and it is exactly why he
> demands confirmation: the P tells you who arrived, not which way the next
> break goes. Note that volume is part of the confirmation. It is closes *with*
> volume beneath them; a close on thin volume is not the signal.

**The counting rules** *(08:56)*

> "Then we use the rules for counting closing prices, which are necessary to
> determine whether the trend continuation will happen or not."

> **Editor —** Invoked three times and never stated. How many closes, on what
> timeframe, how far beyond the edge: none of it is in this video or anywhere
> in the nineteen we swept. It is the load-bearing gap in the framework and the
> reason this is a map rather than a strategy.

**The scenarios** *(09:13)*

> "Let's assume this is now a setup that can form here, so below the range.
> What do I expect there? I expect the market to move downwards again out of
> equilibrium. But how can I pinpoint that? So, if I have a setup there that
> looks like this and the market goes below the range and then back in, yes,
> then the probability is initially high that it will go back up. If the market
> gives me a setup below there that looks like this and breaks out upwards, the
> probability is high that it will go up."

**The break-in, and the reversal** *(09:47)*

> "So, if I am right here at this exact price area, let me make this a bit
> clearer, the market drops down and gives me a P. And now I have the
> opportunity to recognize that buying is happening again. Yes, then it will
> continue to go up. We call this a break-in. But now, there's also the
> following possibility that the market suddenly goes back down into the range
> and moves lower. Oh, so there is a possibility for a trend reversal via
> the P."

> **Editor —** The corpus had the failed breakout as a concept and no name for
> it. Carmine's ep. 6 says to look for the reversal on the first test of a
> breakout almost every time; [HySZZSjMxF8](../HySZZSjMxF8/CARD.md) enters the
> failed break on re-acceptance back inside the range. "Break-in" is the word
> for that event, seen from inside the range. The term reaches us through an AI
> dub of German audio, so hold the word loosely and the idea firmly.

**The target** *(10:17)*

> "Or I have the setup like this, and I get confirmation by the closing price
> that we are moving downwards. So, I know my next target is below the start of
> the impulse. Then we can identify for ourselves that even though there was a
> bullish setup here, we can now plan a trade up to this point."

> **Editor —** The only target rule in the video, and a good one: a failed P is
> not merely faded, it is expected to give back the whole impulse that built
> it. Compare Carmine, whose target is always the opposing level, and this
> channel's own [1utSjHs_Mq8](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md), where re-entry into a
> value area targets the full profile. All three are one instinct: a failed
> auction retraces to where it started.

**Which letter, and when** *(10:49)*

> "You can simply apply them at price levels where you identify, do I now
> expect a trend continuation, or do I rather expect the trend to end here? And
> then you use this P setup or the b setup for exactly the corresponding
> structure."

**The D** *(11:06)*

> "There's also the D setup. And the D setup is nothing more than a profile
> that the market has formed, which looks like this, for example. And through
> the closing price count, we notice that our continuations are no longer
> happening. We get stopped out, maybe once here and once there. Now, the
> market starts to form a balance, that the market goes short at the top and
> long at the bottom, because market participants simply no longer find
> acceptance here. So, you can use the patterns to re-establish trend reversals
> back into the fair price area, and that's the logic behind it."

> **Editor —** An honest passage. The signal that the regime has changed is
> that the continuation trades stop working: you get stopped out once or twice,
> and that is the D announcing itself. The VWAP video,
> [43JaKHRvxHk](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md), gives the same rule from the indicator
> side: classify structure first, trade pullbacks in a trend and the edges in
> balance. Neither video tells you how to see the D before the second stop.
