# Read: This One Candle Close Confirms The Trend Is Still Alive — BOS Explained

> **A Break of Structure is a candle close, never a wick, beyond the most recent swing high; the retest of the broken level is the entry; and at every break the only question is which swing point just broke. The clearest statement in the corpus of why the close-rule camp waits for the close.**

- **Channel:** Smart Money Decode X, Zero to Advanced Trader series, video 10 · **Uploaded:** 2026-08-26 · **Duration:** 11:55
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YwqfGuktXrc

## In brief

The last video of the series and the mirror of the one before it. Video 9 defined the Change of Character as a close below the most recent higher low, the first evidence that a trend is ending. This one defines the Break of Structure as a close above the most recent swing high, the evidence that it is not: the pullback was just a pullback.

The case for the close rule is the most explicit in the corpus. A wick means buyers reached the level and were pushed back before the period ended; a close means they held it. Count wicks and you have a signal on almost every candle.

From there he grades the break by body, volume and the pullback before it, tells you to buy the retest of the broken level rather than the break candle, with a stop just below, stacks daily, four-hour and one-hour charts into one setup, and leaves you watching two levels at all times. There are no numbers anywhere in the video.

## The argument

The video opens with the trader's actual problem. You are long, the structure is clear, and price pulls back. Is this the end, should you get out? Then price recovers and closes above the previous swing high, and that moment is the Break of Structure: the confirmation that the trend has refuelled. He frames it as evidence against prediction; "in trading, evidence always beats speculation."

The definition is strict about which swing counts. A bullish BOS is a close above the most recent swing high, the last peak formed before the pullback began, not any older high. The bearish case is the same rule inverted. Each BOS in the trend direction is the dominant side proving it is still dominant.

Then the passage that gives the video its title. A wick above the swing high is not a BOS; only a close qualifies. His reasoning is about who won the negotiation: a wick says sellers pushed back before the period ended, a close says buyers held it and "won the final verdict." That is the language of [video 4](../oktlv1rOG9Q/CARD.md), where the close was already named the most important price on the candle. The practical argument is noise: price wicks through previous highs constantly, and counting those would put a signal on almost every candle.

This is the corpus's standing dispute, stated from the close side. Carmine Rosato takes the opposite position in [his entry and exit episode](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md): confirmation lives in the live tape, and waiting for a five-minute close moved his stop from two points to seven on a worked ES trade. The Vorwald channel stands with this video, reading acceptance from closes rather than wicks in [its PbD explainer](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md) and requiring a close above consolidation in [its 80 to 90% win-rate video](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md). Two schools read closes and one trades the print, and neither side has published the statistics that would settle it.

Not every BOS is equal. A strong one has a large full-bodied break candle that closes well beyond the level, expanding volume on that candle, and a clean pullback to a logical level before it. A weak one barely closes over, on low volume, after a messy pullback that "spent too long grinding at the support level." A weak BOS needs a second confirmation, and that confirmation is the retest.

The retest is the entry model. After a genuine break, price often returns to the level it broke, and he calls this one of the highest-probability entries in trend trading, for two reasons at once: the old resistance is being tested as support, and the order block that produced the break is being approached from above. The trader who enters there has a defined level, an invalidation point just below it, and a tight stop. If the level breaks, the BOS was false.

Here the schools converge more than the close dispute suggests. Carmine's [volume episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md) says never buy the initial break, buy the pullback, and his [support and resistance episode](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md) lists break-then-retest as his first way to play a level. Where they part is on what proves the retest is holding. This video takes a one-hour trigger candle at the level; Carmine wants to see aggression exhausting into passive size on the tape. Both put the stop where the thesis is wrong.

The BOS and the ChoCH are then set side by side as mirror images: in a bullish trend the BOS breaks the most recent high and means continuation, the ChoCH breaks the most recent low and means potential reversal. The one question in real time is which swing point just broke. The multi-timeframe stack repeats the [series framework](../EW_TtV_rgzY/CARD.md): a daily BOS gives direction, the four-hour retest gives the entry, the one-hour chart gives the trigger candle. A BOS aligned across timeframes is multiplied in significance; a one-minute BOS in the daily direction is "slightly relevant."

Three mistakes close the teaching: calling a wick a BOS while the candle is still forming, entering on the break candle instead of the retest, and treating a four-hour BOS against the daily trend as equal to one aligned with it. The final image is the two-level watch: the most recent swing high for continuation, the most recent swing low for reversal. This is [video 7's](../dAlP8UZXT48/CARD.md) structure made operational, and it depends on the swing points having been identified correctly by [video 8's](../d2RzSJC98o8/CARD.md) rules.

What is missing is what the whole series is missing: any number. No stop distance, no target, no risk-reward, no definition of "few ticks" or "expanding volume," no hit rate for the retest entry. The nearest figures in the corpus are the Vorwald channel's two to three hundred trades before a statistic means anything, and its worked example of a 50% hit rate with a risk-reward of "maybe a two."

## In his words

**The situation every trend trader faces** *(00:00)*

> "Price has been moving in your direction. You are positioned correctly. The structure is clear, and then price pulls back. And in that pullback, doubt creeps in. Is this the end of the trend? Is this a change of character? Should I exit now before it gets worse? And then price recovers and breaks above the previous swing high, and the trend continues. That moment when price closes above the previous swing high, that is a break of structure, and it is the single most powerful confirmation that a trend is continuing that exists in all of market structure analysis."

**Evidence, not prediction** *(00:51)*

> "In video 9, we learned that a change of character is the first structural evidence that a trend is ending: a close below the most recent higher low in a bullish trend. The sellers broke through a level the buyers previously defended. That was the warning. The break of structure is the opposite signal. It is the confirmation that the side in control is still in control. That the pullback was just a pullback. That the trend has refueled and is ready to continue. Every professional trend trader waits for the break of structure before adding to positions or entering new ones. Because the break of structure is evidence, not prediction. It tells you what has already happened, not what might happen. And in trading, evidence always beats speculation."

> **Editor —** "Every professional trend trader waits" is asserted, not shown; the corpus holds at least one professional who does not. Carmine Rosato's confirmation is the order printing now, and he treats waiting for the candle as the mistake. Hold that against the next section.

**What a break of structure actually is** *(01:36)*

> "A break of structure in a bullish trend occurs when price closes above the most recent swing high. Not any swing high, the most recent one, the last peak that was formed before the pullback began. When price closes above that specific level, it has done two things simultaneously. It has confirmed that the current pullback is over, and it has confirmed that the buyers had enough conviction to surpass the previous high, which means the upward sequence of higher highs is continuing. The structure is intact. The trend is alive.
>
> In a bearish trend, a break of structure occurs when price closes below the most recent swing low. [...] Every time you see a break of structure in the direction of the established trend, you are watching the dominant side prove they are still dominant."

**The close requirement, why it matters every single time** *(02:34)*

> "A wick above the most recent swing high is not a break of structure. A candle that touches the swing high level and pulls back is not a break of structure. Only a candle close above the swing high qualifies. We covered this in video 9 from the change of character perspective. The same logic applies here with equal force. A wick tells you buyers reached that level, but sellers pushed back before the period ended. The sellers won that specific negotiation. A close above tells you buyers reached that level and held it when the period ended. The sellers could not push back enough to prevent the close above. The buyers won the final verdict.
>
> This distinction removes an enormous amount of noise from your structural analysis. On any active chart, you will see price frequently touching and briefly exceeding previous swing highs with wicks before pulling back. If you counted every wick as a break of structure, you would be seeing break of structure signals on almost every candle. The close requirement filters out the noise and keeps only the signals that actually represent a genuine shift in the balance of power at the structural level."

> **Editor —** This is the corpus's dispute in one paragraph. The Vorwald channel would sign it: acceptance is read from where candles close, never from where they wick, in [its PbD explainer](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md), and the entry there and in [its win-rate video](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md) requires a close, not a wick. Carmine's [entry episode](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md) answers the noise argument directly: a wick is not noise if the tape shows who made it, and the price of waiting for the period to end was seven points of stop instead of two on his worked trade. Notice what the two sides actually disagree about. Both agree the wick alone tells you nothing. The close camp resolves it by waiting; Carmine resolves it by reading the volume that made the wick. Neither has published a hit rate.

**What makes a break strong** *(03:48)*

> "A strong break of structure has three characteristics. The break candle is large and decisive. A full-bodied candle that closes well above the swing high, not just barely above it. The body of the candle extends significantly beyond the swing high level. Volume on the break candle is expanding. More participation than on recent candles means the buying is conviction-driven. Institutional participation is visible through the size and volume of the break candle. And the break follows a clean, well-defined pullback to a logical level, such as an order block, a fair value gap, or the most recent swing low. The retracement that preceded the break was orderly and structured, not a chaotic, choppy grind that spent too long at the support area."

> **Editor —** Two of the three are relative judgements with no number attached, and the card records that none is given. The second characteristic is where Carmine would object most. In [his volume episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md) a breakout on heavy aggressive volume that gets no follow-through is the setup he fades, so a large candle on expanding volume is, to him, the question rather than the answer. The Vorwald channel's version is closer to this one: closes with volume beneath them confirm the break, a close on thin volume does not. And "institutional participation is visible" is an inference. A candle records that volume traded, not who traded it.

**What makes a break weak** *(04:34)*

> "A weak break of structure has opposite characteristics. The break candle barely closes above the swing high, just a few ticks [...] above the level. Volume is not expanding. Low participation suggests the break lacks conviction. And the break happens after a messy pullback that spent too long grinding at the support level, suggesting the buyers defending that area are weakening rather than consolidating for the next push. A strong break of structure is a high quality signal to enter with the trend. A weak break of structure is a caution signal. It may still lead to trend continuation, but it requires additional confirmation before entering. And the additional confirmation is the retest, which we will cover shortly."

**The retest after a break of structure** *(05:17)*

> "After a genuine break of structure, price frequently returns to retest the level it just broke. In a bullish trend, after price closes above the most recent swing high, it often pulls back to that broken swing high level before continuing higher. This pullback is the retest. And the retest is one of the highest probability entry points in all of trend trading. Here is why. The broken swing high is now a structural level with a specific mechanical meaning. It was the previous peak where sellers once had control. Price broke above it and closed there. Now when price returns to that level from above, two things are happening simultaneously. The level that was once resistance is being tested as new support, and the order block that created the break of structure, the cluster of buying that drove price above the swing high, is being approached from above. Both factors create buying pressure at the retest level."

> **Editor —** Note his own hedges: "frequently", "often", "one of the highest probability". The slide behind him reads "THE RETEST = HIGHEST PROBABILITY ENTRY IN TREND TRADING", which is harder than what he says. Keep the spoken version. On the substance, Carmine agrees about the entry and disagrees about the mechanism. Break then retest is the first of his three ways to play a level in [the support and resistance episode](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md), but there a level only becomes support because the order flow shows aggression exhausting into passive size on the retest; "resistance becomes support" is not a property of the line. This video treats the flip and the order block as reasons buyers will be there. Carmine would want to see them.

**Why the retest entry is the one to take** *(06:12)*

> "A trader who enters at the retest of a broken swing high in a bullish trend has several advantages. Their entry is at a defined structural level with a clear invalidation point just below the retest level. Their stop loss is tight and logical. The level must hold for the break of structure to be genuine. If it breaks, the break of structure was false and the stop protects them from holding a bad position. And their potential reward is the continuation of the established trend from the highest probability entry point within it."

> **Editor —** The principle underneath is one all three schools sign: the stop belongs where the thesis is wrong. What no one in this series ever says is how far below the level that is, what the target is, or what the pair of them gives as a risk-reward. The corpus's only such figures are the Vorwald channel's, in [its statistics video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md): two to three hundred trades before a statistic means anything, and a worked example at a 50% hit rate giving "maybe a two".

**The only question** *(07:44)*

> "One breaks the high, one breaks the low. Same trend, opposite meanings. The only question you need to ask in real time is this. Which swing point just broke? If a high broke, it is a break of structure. Continuation. If a low broke, it is a change of character. Potential reversal. That single question, applied consistently to every break you observe, gives you the structural clarity that most retail traders never develop."

> **Editor —** The question only works if the swing points were drawn correctly, which is [video 8's](../d2RzSJC98o8/CARD.md) job, and the ChoCH half is [video 9's](../iPDi9_nzn-o/CARD.md), which adds the caution this video does not repeat: a break that snaps back within a candle or two is a sweep, not a change of character.

**Reading break of structure on multiple timeframes** *(08:06)*

> "A break of structure on the daily chart is one of the most significant signals in market structure analysis. It tells you the highest level participants in the market, the institutions trading the daily time frame, have pushed price beyond the previous peak. That is not a signal to ignore. When you see a daily break of structure, you drop to the 4-hour chart and look for the retest. The daily break of structure established the direction. The 4-hour retest gives you the entry, and the 1-hour chart gives you the specific trigger candle at the retest level. [...] The significance of a break of structure is multiplied when it aligns across multiple time frames. A one minute break of structure in the direction of the daily trend is slightly relevant. A daily break of structure confirmed by a 4-hour retest with a 1-hour entry candle is a complete multi-time frame setup with full structural context behind it."

> **Editor —** The stack is [video 5's](../EW_TtV_rgzY/CARD.md) daily-structure, four-hour-setup, one-hour-entry framework applied to one signal. "The institutions trading the daily time frame" is again an attribution the chart cannot support; a daily close says that price closed there, not which participants closed it. The Vorwald podcast in [the order-flow interview](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md) is impatient with exactly this habit of narrating institutional intent onto price, while conceding the mechanical effects.

**The most common mistakes** *(09:17)*

> "The first mistake is treating a wick above the swing high as a break of structure. [...] Only a close qualifies, but it bears repeating because traders make this mistake constantly, especially in real-time analysis when the candle is still forming. A candle that is currently above the swing high with 30 seconds remaining in the period is not a break of structure yet. Wait for the close.
>
> The second mistake is entering immediately on the break candle without waiting for the retest. The break candle itself is often extended, and buying at the high of a break candle means buying into a level where sellers are likely to step in and push back. The retest gives you a better entry with a tighter stop. Patience after the break is rewarded more than aggression on the break.
>
> The third mistake is treating every break of structure as equal regardless of time frame context. A break of structure on the 4-hour chart against a bearish daily trend is not the same signal as a break of structure on the 4-hour chart aligned with a bullish daily trend. Context from the higher time frame determines the significance of every break of structure on the lower time frame. Trade breaks of structure that align with the higher time frame trend. Avoid breaks that go against it."

> **Editor —** The second mistake is the one thing every school in this corpus agrees on. Carmine's rule is never to buy the initial break; the Vorwald channel's is a pullback after the breakout. The first mistake is the one Carmine rejects outright, and his reason is the same as this presenter's reason for the second: buying late means buying extended, with a wider stop. Carmine simply applies that logic to the close as well.

**Putting the two together** *(10:44)*

> "In a bullish trend, you are watching two levels simultaneously. The most recent swing high for the break of structure and the most recent swing low for the change of character. As long as price makes new break of structures without triggering a change of character, the trend is intact, and you look for long opportunities at the retests of each break. The moment a change of character triggers before a new break of structure, the trend may be changing, and you stop looking for longs until the structure clarifies. This dual level watch creates a complete structural framework. You always know the two levels that matter most. The one that confirms continuation and the one that warns of reversal. Between those two levels, the market is giving you real time structural information with every candle. And a trader who reads that information fluently, using correctly identified swing points and the close-only qualification rule, has a structural understanding of the market that most retail traders never achieve."

> **Editor —** This is the cleanest operating statement in the ten videos, and it is the step [video 7](../dAlP8UZXT48/CARD.md) called watching the most recent swing low, made into a standing rule. It is also a rule with no exit: the trend is intact until a ChoCH, and a ChoCH is only a warning until video 9's second confirmation arrives. A trader who follows it will be long through the first leg of every reversal by design. Whether the retest entries pay for that is the statistic the series never gives.
