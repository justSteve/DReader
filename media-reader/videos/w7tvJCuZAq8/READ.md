# Read: You've Been Using Volume WRONG This Whole Time...

> **Carmine's two volume frameworks: fade the first test of a breakout when aggressive volume gets no follow-through, and buy the pullback that absorbs it, never the break itself.**

- **Channel:** Carmine Rosato, "Trading Orderflow Series" ep. 6 · **Uploaded:** 2024-11-13 · **Duration:** 31:28
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w7tvJCuZAq8

## In brief

A volume bar on a candlestick chart tells you that something traded. It does
not tell you who was buying, who was selling, or whether the aggressor got what
they wanted. Carmine's argument is that retail reads that bar as confirmation,
buys the breakout it sits under, and becomes the liquidity a patient seller was
waiting for.

His alternative is volume by price: footprint, heatmap, depth of market. Read
that way, the same spike separates into two setups. When aggressive buying piles
into a level and price does not lift, a passive seller is absorbing it and the
breakout should be faded; he looks for that reversal on the first test 99% of
the time. When the spike arrives on a pullback inside a trend, it is the crowd
being stopped out, and the pullback is the entry. The rule that binds the two:
never buy the initial break. Let it break, then buy the retest, because a
defended retest proves the participants. It is the most quoted episode in the
series, and the corpus argues with it.

## The argument

He starts from a failure everyone has had: volume confirms the breakout, you
buy it, it reverses, you are stopped out. His diagnosis is that the confirmation
was never there. A volume bar is volume by time: how much traded inside a
candle, nothing about who initiated it. Volume by price, on the footprint,
heatmap or depth of market, shows whether that volume hit the ask or the bid and
whether price moved for it. Every example in the episode is one spike read both
ways.

The first framework is the high volume reversal. The market moves up until the
last buyer buys. If aggressive buying is pouring into a level, visible as
positive delta, and price is not lifting, a passive seller is taking the other
side and the buyers are being absorbed. His opening example is a strong opening
candle into the previous day's high with a volume spike beneath it; the
footprint for that half hour shows cells like 1775 contracts with +1319 delta and
a 620-contract cell entirely at the ask, and the market went nowhere. The
support version is the mirror: the biggest volume bar of the selloff prints on
the break, and the bar directly after it is the low, because the last seller has
sold into a buyer who was waiting.

This is where ep. 5 ([5qBo04SMUFc](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md)) lives inside ep. 6.
That episode argued that a level is only valid when order flow validates it;
this one supplies the volume mechanics: tails at the extreme, aggressive delta
with no follow-through. The absorption read itself is the corpus's strongest convergence: ep. 2's
trapped-traders slide ([eJZhX6Xz4cU](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md)), and on the
Vorwald channel a buyer repeatedly lifting a level while price fails to advance
([usho6UVLqkE](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md)), arrived at separately.

He then concedes that markets do continue, and says of the S&P that reversal
happens "more than 50% of the time". It is an estimate, and the only regime
statement in the series. The Vorwald channel puts numbers on the same intuition,
balance about 70% of the time, and in its VWAP video
([43JaKHRvxHk](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md)) turns it into a rule: classify structure
first; trend means a pull-back entry, balance means a mean-reversion target.
Carmine has no classifier. His switch is sequential: fade the first test, then
trade continuation only once a break has been made and defended.

The second framework, continuation, is what that defended retest looks like on
the tape. In a trend, the volume spike on a pullback is the crowd shorting a
trendline break and being stopped out, and their covering is the spike. The
footprint on the pullback is red, aggressive selling, and price holds anyway, so
a passive buyer is present; at the low the last print is 178 contracts, all
sold, and the pullback ends. Hence the rule: never buy the initial break. Let
it break, which proves the buyers were strong, then buy the pullback when the
flipped level is defended, which proves they are stronger. In the worked example
the retest of a flipped resistance prints the session's largest negative deltas,
−750 and −538, with no breakdown. "Largest of the session so far" is his entire
threshold. There is no number, here or in ep. 3's "just an OUTLIER in the data"
([tgQC7Dpcc8A](../tgQC7Dpcc8A/CARD.md)). The Vorwald channel puts numbers on
neighbouring quantities, resting ES size above 250–300 contracts
([Vd83oo_geMk](../Vd83oo_geMk/CARD.md)), 200–300 trades before a statistic
counts, a worked example at a 50% hit rate with an average risk-reward of
"maybe a two" ([NkQeOVDTAec](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md)), but no delta threshold
either.

The break-and-retest rule is the most contested line in the corpus, and the
contest is not where it was first filed. NkQeOVDTAec, on the Vorwald channel,
teaches the pullback after the breakout and agrees with Carmine.
[zAwEX_tRUfE](../zAwEX_tRUfE/CARD.md), same channel, enters on the initial break
above the prior-day Value Area High. The rule for choosing sits in the VWAP video
and is stated in neither entry video. That is an internal inconsistency on that
channel, not a Carmine-versus-Vorwald split.

His covering mechanism needs no hunter: retail shorts the break, retail is
stopped, retail's buying is the spike. That is nearer Trading Notes' on-screen
"NOBODY IS HUNTING YOUR STOPS" ([IUWvHVout94](../IUWvHVout94/CARD.md)) than the
sweep school, and not what the Vorwald channel dismisses as the stop-hunt myth
([RwQBdF9TSvc](../RwQBdF9TSvc/CARD.md)), which is institutional size below lows.

The close is about location. Rather than buy the break, he wants to be long
before it, on the absorbed pullback, with the level as target. He draws two
boxes, pullback entry against breakout entry; the later one has a shorter
reward and a stop that must sit under the whole base. No numbers are written.
Ep. 7 ([7facFfjQ0UE](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md)) supplies them, a 2-point
stop against about 7, and states the rule this episode is the confirmation half
of: Context, Location, Confirmation, read on the live tape, never on a candle
close. zAwEX_tRUfE reaches the same place by arithmetic: a mid-range entry at a
risk-reward around 0.8 needs roughly 56% just to break even.
