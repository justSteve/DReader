# Read: How To Enter and Exit Trades Like a Professional

> **The CLC rule stated in full — context, location, confirmation — and the case that confirmation lives in the live tape, never in a candle close, because waiting for the close turns a two-point stop into a seven-point one.**

- **Channel:** Carmine Rosato, Trading Orderflow Series ep. 7 · **Uploaded:** 2024-11-18 · **Duration:** 32:19
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7facFfjQ0UE

## In brief

This is where Carmine says what actually makes him click the button. He calls it the CLC rule. Context is the story the market is telling: the trend, and whether today's volume is unusually heavy or light. Location is a level where the auction should react, a supply or demand zone, support or resistance. Confirmation is the question he asks once price is there: are buyers really buying, or are they trapped? Are sellers selling, or trapped?

The first two he reads off candlestick charts, and he lists the timeframes he uses. The third he refuses to take from any candle. Confirmation comes from what he calls the Now: the orders printing at this moment. Candles are made of volume, so the volume knows first, and a trader who waits for a five-minute close has moved his stop from two points to seven before he is even in.

Two journaled ES trades, a long and a short, are replayed bar by bar to prove it.

## The argument

The rule takes him five minutes to state and everything else hangs from it. Context: what is the trend, higher highs and higher lows or the reverse, and how does today's volume compare with normal? Location: is price at a level where he is "comfortable knowing that the auction should react"? Confirmation: at that level, are buyers buying or trapped, sellers selling or trapped? Context and location come from time-based charts, and he lists them. Confirmation comes only from the flow.

The reason is a claim about causation. A green candle prints because there was more aggressive buying than passive selling. "Candles form because of the volume. The volume doesn't form because of the candles." So a candlestick chart can tell him where he is but never when. The timeframe he trades is the Now. If the market breaks support and the prints show buying, he is long at that moment, not after the rally. If it breaks resistance and a large seller just above gets filled, he is short as the seller fills. And if the Now does not give him enough, "that is information in itself" and he stands aside.

The two trades are one read, mirrored. On 3 October 2024 the ES pulls back into demand in an uptrend; inside the zone a footprint cell shows 253 contracts with a delta of −253, every one sold at the bid, then over 900 more sell contracts in about five seconds as the low breaks, and price ticks up. Someone is buying all of it. He is long at 5740 with the stop under that seller-who-is-really-a-buyer: 14.5 points on 30 contracts. On 11 November the market rallies off overnight selling into supply near 6045; the delta builds to +3,300 against a resting offer that will not move, the high of day breaks by one tick with 1,140 contracts hitting the ask and nothing on the bid, and price goes nowhere. Short at 6044, stop a point over the high: 11.5 points on 40 contracts.

Then the risk argument. Enter the October trade on the five-minute close after the trend line breaks, and you are long near 5746. The thesis is only wrong below the low of day, so the honest stop is 5739: seven points of risk to a first target seven points away, one to one. Enter on the print at 5740 with a generous two-point stop and the same target is twelve points off, six to one or better. "Entry is everything." Where the stop goes is not in dispute; it goes where the thesis fails. What the close does is move you seven points further from that place.

This is the episode that lets us set Carmine beside the Vorwald channel (Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge), because [its volume-profile video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) teaches the same two-step under different names. The profile gives an "idea", a level where a reaction is possible; a local setup gives the "proof" that it is happening now. Idea is Carmine's location, proof is his confirmation, and neither cites the other. The split is over what counts as proof. The Vorwald presenter accepts a chart formation, and asks for a candle close: closes rather than wicks in [the PbD strategy video](../Ly62G168MkQ/CARD.md), closes outside the value area in [the German PbD video](../jXR1afMy-3o/CARD.md), a close above consolidation in [the 80–90% win-rate video](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md). The [Smart Money Decode X series](../../playlists/smdx-zero-to-advanced-PLU9kIorYkc18.md) sits on the Vorwald side too, with its close-below-the-most-recent-higher-low trigger. On this point the corpus has two camps, and this episode is the clearest statement of the minority one.

What Carmine still never gives is the number that would settle it. He concedes the price of tight stops out loud, "this enables me to lose a lot more and be incorrect a lot more", and never says how much more. No win rate, no delta threshold, no sizing rule beyond thirty or forty contracts, in any of the eight episodes. The Vorwald video supplies what he leaves out: two to three hundred trades before your statistics mean anything, and a worked example at a 50% hit rate giving an average risk-reward of "maybe a two". Carmine's journal multiples of 9.67R and 7.67R look extraordinary, and the reason for caution is that across the series he shows seven winners and no losers. A curated sample, not a track record.

Two smaller connections. Both trades are re-shown on a Bookmap heatmap, the passive participant as a band and the aggression as bubbles hitting it; the Vorwald channel's [heatmap video](../Vd83oo_geMk/CARD.md) says institutions do not rely on that picture, because it shows where limit orders sat, not intent. And the October trade is the session [episode 2](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md) logged from its own footprint, with the same −735 and −1171 deltas at 5740: two episodes agreeing on one tape, which is free verification.

**Context** *(01:57)*

> "Whenever you hear me say context, what I really mean is: what is the trend? Are we uptrending, are we putting in higher highs and higher lows? Is the market downtrending, is it putting in lower lows and lower highs? What is the trend, and how is today's volume? Is today's volume significantly higher, is it significantly lower? Are there buyers or are there sellers factored in at a high or low? Just what is the context.
>
> The way I view the charts and the way I view the market is, it's a storyteller, and our job is to read the story the market is telling. And if we don't know truly what the story is, well then we have to use context clues to put the pieces together to figure out the story."

**Location** *(03:26)*

> "Point number two of the CLC rule is location. Is it at a level of interest? The basis to my trading strategy revolves around trading the market at significant levels of interest. Where is the location of the market? Is it at a support/resistance level, is it at a supply or a demand zone? Where is the location that the market is trading at, and is it at a level of interest that I am comfortable knowing that the auction should react to this location of interest?"

**Confirmation, and why a candle cannot supply it** *(04:39)*

> "Point number three of the CLC rule is confirmation. Are buyers willing to buy at my level? Are buyers trapped? Are sellers willing to sell at my level of interest, or are sellers trapped? So confirmation is the last portion of the CLC rule, because this is what makes me click the buy or sell button, and this is what makes me exit or enter that specific trade.
>
> The market moves up simply because there is more aggressive buying than there is passive selling. That's the only way the market could move up. … I don't want traders to have the misunderstanding that just because we have a green candle to the upside means that more buying is coming in. More buying comes in, which then is printed on the candlestick chart. The candles form because of the volume; the volume doesn't form because of the candles. Do not get that twisted.
>
> Because of this, a candlestick chart does not tell me when to enter or exit. The tape does, the volume does, the order flow does. I am never making a sole trading decision off of a candlestick chart. … Footprint charts, a heat map, a depth of market, any tool which enables me to read the volume and the order flow properly will let me see what's going on behind the candlestick charts, and then I could spot how strong or how weak the buyers or sellers are, and even where there's a large buyer or seller present, or where there's absorption."

> **Editor —** This is the load-bearing claim of the episode, and it is a claim about lag, not about candles being wrong. A candle is a summary of flow that has already happened; the flow is available before the summary is. The Vorwald channel's own definition of order flow, "who is currently buying, who is currently selling, and how aggressively", is the same idea from the other side.

**What the timeframe charts are for** *(06:53)*

> "That's not to say I don't use candlestick charts or any time-based chart at all. Context and location of the CLC rule most of the time, not always, comes from a candlestick chart. So the timeframes that I will use, if I am using a candlestick chart for context and location — not confirmation, because confirmation I use the volume for — for context and location I'll use these timeframes."

> **Editor —** The slide, verified against the frame: one day of 1-minute bars; 15 days of 5-minute; 30 days of 15-minute; 90 days of 30-minute; 360 days each of 1-hour, 90-minute, 2-hour, 3-hour and 4-hour; one year of daily; three years of weekly. Context and location only.

**The Now** *(07:38)*

> "For me personally, I will never click the buy or sell button strictly off of a candlestick chart. I need to read the order flow, and the only way we can do this is what I call the Now. … I use the timeframe of the Now when I am reading the orders coming in right now, in the present moment. Because right now, in the present moment, the market provides all the information that I need to click the buy or sell button. And if it doesn't provide me enough information, then that is information in itself, for me to avoid a trade and to not take that specific setup.
>
> If I see the market breaking below a support level … but as soon as it breaks there's buying activity here — well, guess what, I'm getting long as soon as I see the buying activity. I'm not waiting for the market to rally up and getting long all the way up here. … Or if the market is breaking this level of resistance and there's a massive seller sitting right here above resistance — well, as soon as I see that seller get filled, I am going to get short. … I want to get short when the sellers are present, not when the market has already sold off."

> **Editor —** Here the two camps in the corpus separate. The Vorwald presenter's "proof" can be a formation and, repeatedly, a candle close. Carmine's cannot: the seller getting filled *is* the proof, and by the close it is seven points behind you. The principle underneath, that the stop belongs where the thesis is wrong, both would sign.
