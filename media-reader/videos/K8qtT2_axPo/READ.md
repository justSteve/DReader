## In brief

Market Profile, he says, is nothing more than a 30-minute chart collapsed back
onto the price axis. Each half hour gets a letter; stack the letters where the
market traded and a balanced day draws a bell curve. From that picture he reads
four things: how the open was structured, where the market spent the most time
(the POC, which he is careful to say is not the volume-weighted VPOC), where it
spent 70% of its time (the value area), and where it barely stopped at all
(single prints, tails).

The claim underneath is a base rate: 70% of the time the market is in balance,
and the big participants need that balance to do their business. So a profile
tells you which regime you are in, where the fair prices are, and which unfair
prices are likely to be tested or repaired. It is context, he insists, not a
strategy: a road map, with the trade coming from somewhere else.

## The argument

He starts from the origin: TPO, Time Price Opportunity, developed by Peter
Steidlmayer, a soybean floor trader. The point is to determine a context for
the day, and the mechanics are deliberately simple. Standardised 30-minute
blocks show where the market had momentum and where it had lethargy.
The first block is the opening range, and its size is the first fact of the
day: a very large open means the participants were in total imbalance from the
start, a very small one is a rubber band that gives a later expansion more
substance behind it. Then the blocks are aggregated by price, and a market
that oscillated all day collapses into a bell curve, the normal or balance
profile. Trend profiles are the other family. This is the same auction picture
Carmine Rosato opens his series with in
[QaNPAaEnB5E](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md), balance as the range where the two sides
agree and imbalance as the event that breaks it, drawn here with time instead
of volume.

The vocabulary is where the video earns its place. The POC is the price where
the market spent the most time, and he stops to say it is not to be confused
with the VPOC, which is volume weighted. No other source in the corpus makes
that cut; Carmine and the Smart Money Decode X series both say "POC" without
saying which, and Trader Dale ([vVMJa7dyYWE](../vVMJa7dyYWE/CARD.md)) means
the heaviest-volume price. The two diverge, and the companion video
[1utSjHs_Mq8](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md) names that divergence the anomaly and
treats it as the tell. The value area is where the market spent 70% of the
main trading time. That is the time-based twin of the volume-profile value
area the channel teaches in [YmygDgtoxO8](../YmygDgtoxO8/CARD.md), where the
edges are framed as the point the market starts moving into imbalance, and
where [aursfDVYzUk](../aursfDVYzUk/CARD.md) gives the basis as 68.2% or 70%.

The rest of the alphabet is about rejection and weakness. A buying tail is a
place the market spent only 30 minutes before shooting up; single letters at
the bottom of a profile are a buying tail, at the top a selling tail, and
both say who dominated that day. This is Carmine's volume tail arrived at
from the other direction: he reads prints thinning to a handful at the
extreme in [5qBo04SMUFc](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md) and
[eJZhX6Xz4cU](../eJZhX6Xz4cU/CARD.md); the profile trader reads a single
half-hour letter. Ledgers, several letters ending at one price, are the
opposite reading: weak hands. A double distribution is two profiles in one
session, two POCs with single prints between them and no fair price traded
in the gap, and his rule is that the market will oscillate and fill that gap
unless it breaks out of the value area. That is one instinct with the
full-profile traverse of [1utSjHs_Mq8](../1utSjHs_Mq8/CARD.md).

The base rate holds the method together: 70% of the time the market is in
balanced sideways phases, and it dips below or pokes above only to come back,
because the big participants need price discovery to position themselves at
fair results. He gives no source. The channel repeats the number as the share
of phases that return to fair value in [YmygDgtoxO8](../YmygDgtoxO8/CARD.md),
Trader Dale states the same 70% for rotation, also unsourced, and the same
channel's VWAP video ([VumVuGnCcFM](../VumVuGnCcFM/CARD.md)) puts strong trend
days at more than 20%, which sits awkwardly beside a 30% imbalance share.
Nobody in the corpus has measured it. What the number buys him is the regime
split behind PbD: the D of [m3IMdc7QwN4](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md) is his
balance phase and this video's normal profile; the P and b are his aggressive
trend phases, built from this video's trend profiles and single prints.

Then the disclaimer, which he means. This is not a self-contained trading
strategy but a root planning. Normal or trend profile on the left, where the
strong hands were, will they defend those levels again or will they break:
these are questions the profile poses and the volume answers, which is where
he says to overlay the volume profile next. If the market accepts prices
outside value, it gets dangerous and the break is probable; if it rejects
them and comes back, the trend continues. That is the same test Carmine
applies to a level in [5qBo04SMUFc](../5qBo04SMUFc/CARD.md), real only once
the market confirms it, and it is the Idea half of the channel's Idea and
Proof entry model in [NkQeOVDTAec](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md), a level from the
profile awaiting a local setup. The closing image is the order-flow question
in [vl01TiVTuoQ](../vl01TiVTuoQ/CARD.md) asked of a slower instrument: who is
dominating the market, and from which point the dominance ends.

Nothing here is a trade: no entry, no stop, no target distance, no P&L. The
one conditional forecast, that a very large open followed by a move to new
fair prices will be tested back, is stated with "very often" and no figure.

## In his words

**Building the profile** *(02:47)*

> "I can collapse all this information to get a nice overview. [...] Nothing

**The double distribution** *(11:04)*

> "This is called a double distribution day. Essentially, these are two
> profiles in one. Very important: there must be what's called a single print
> here for it to be genuine. What does that mean? Single prints are always
> individual letters within a profile. And such single prints immediately tell
> us that there is a high probability the market has currently established a
> trend profile. Or simply put, it's a trend profile. [...] What we have here,
> in effect, are two so-called POCs. And these two POCs give us the
> information that the market has built two profiles, and in between them no
> fair prices have ever been traded. This means that the price is accepted up
> here and down here."

> "The rules are relatively easy. There is a high probability that the market
> will first try to establish this back-and-forth movement, unless it breaks
> out of the value area to the upside or downside, and it will try to fill in
> these unfair price levels here, so that the whole thing can potentially
> become a large normal distribution later on. Because these market
> participants up here, and those who join in afterwards, they will try to
> fill this empty space here with transactions in order to bring stability
> and achieve a fair price discovery again." *(12:00)*

> **Editor —** The single print defined here, a letter the market left within
> one half hour, is the object [m3IMdc7QwN4](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md) reads as a
