# Read: My Simple 1-Minute Scalping Strategy for $10,000/Month

> **The scalp most people take, priced: bought inside yesterday's value area it pays about 0.8 to 1 and needs a win rate near 60% just to break even. The fix is to wait for the break above the prior day's Value Area High, and that is the trade he never prices.**

- **Channel:** Tom Vorwald EN / WorldClassEdge · **Uploaded:** 2026-09-04 · **Duration:** 10:38
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zAwEX_tRUfE

## In brief

He is arguing that scalpers on the 1-minute chart do not lose because their
signals are bad. They lose because of where they take them. A good formation
bought in the middle of yesterday's balance has the upper edge of that balance
sitting on top of it, so the profit is capped at a couple of points while the
stop under the last low is wider than that. He puts the risk-reward at roughly
0.8 and says it needs a win rate of "at least a good 60%" to tread water.

The alternative is one line on the chart: the prior day's Value Area High,
read off a market profile before the session starts. Above it the market is
leaving equilibrium, the run is longer, and the stop is the moment price is
accepted back inside. No indicators, no live tape, one level.

Why it matters to us: this is the only place in the corpus where trade
location is turned into arithmetic rather than asserted. The weakness is that
the arithmetic stops exactly where the recommendation begins.

## The argument

He opens on a chart of the S&P 500, points at a P-formation from what he calls
the PbD structure, and says yes, that is a setup, you can use it. Then he asks
the question the video is actually about: if you buy the close at 7482, what is
in the way? Not the prior highs. Large participants "care relatively little
about that". What is in the way is yesterday's balance. He never expands PbD
here; the letters get their meaning in
[the crisis video](../m3IMdc7QwN4/CARD.md), where a P is an aggressive trend
phase upward and a D a balance phase.

The context comes from a market profile of the previous cash session. He
reads it the way the channel's
[market-profile video](../K8qtT2_axPo/CARD.md) teaches: where the market
opened, where it ran, where it balanced, and a single-print area where strength
is to be expected. The point that matters is that the entry at 7482 sits below
the equilibrium at 7485. The market "always strives to stay in equilibrium at
first", so the honest target is the upper edge of value, not the moon.

Then he prices it. Target a couple of points above, stop under the last low,
and "the loss distance compared to the profit distance is even larger". Risk-
reward "somewhere in the range of 0.8", which he says needs a win rate of at
least a good 60% to break even over many trades. The true break-even at 0.8 is
55.56%, so he is conservative by about four points, and once ES commissions
are counted the rounding is defensible. What makes this passage valuable is
not the precision but the move itself: nobody else in the corpus derives
location quality from a win-rate identity. Carmine shows location with
winners; this presenter shows it with division.

The recommended trade is the mirror image. He draws the prior day's Value Area
High on the screenshot, notes an accumulation station forming just under it
where "significant buying power entered the market", and then marks the break
above the line as "the specific signal that, for you as a short-term 1-minute
scalper, is a truly great technical signal". The reasoning is auction
reasoning: above the line the market is out of equilibrium, so the run is more
predictable; and the risk is defined by acceptance, because "you're out
relatively quickly down here if the market accepts the old equilibrium again".
This is [Carmine's opening premise](../QaNPAaEnB5E/CARD.md), balance and
imbalance, arrived at in different words and on a different platform. Neither
man cites the other.

Where it parts from Carmine is the entry. Carmine's rule in
[the volume episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md) is never buy the initial break;
let it break and buy the pullback. Here the entry is the break. But the
conflict turns out to be inside this channel rather than between sources. The
same presenter teaches pullback-after-breakout as the proof step in
[the statistics video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md), teaches the reversion trade on
re-acceptance in [the multi-timeframe video](../HySZZSjMxF8/CARD.md), and
supplies the rule for choosing in [the VWAP video](../43JaKHRvxHk/CARD.md),
restated in [the earlier VWAP video](../VumVuGnCcFM/CARD.md): classify the
structure first, trend means pull-back entry, balance means mean reversion. By
his own count the market is balanced about 70% of the time, so what this
video teaches is the 30% case, and it never says so.

Then the asymmetry. The rejected trade got a risk-reward and a break-even. The
recommended trade gets "you also have a mathematical advantage, so even with
just a 50 or 60% win rate, you can make money with this", and nothing else: no
stop distance, no target distance, no ratio. At 50% you need a risk-reward of
at least 1.0 for that sentence to be true; he never shows that it is. The
channel's [80 to 90% video](../uFxYcpiaOpw/CARD.md) has the same shape, a hit
rate with no risk beside it. Carmine, for his part, would object to the stop
itself: acceptance back inside is slower than a tape read, and in
[the entries episode](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md) he argues that waiting for that
kind of confirmation is what turns a two-point stop into a seven-point one.

Two smaller things. The platform is ATAS on a dxFeed feed, an order-flow
platform with footprint, delta and DOM, the same category as the Sierra Chart
and Bookmap in [Carmine's tools episode](../tgQC7Dpcc8A/CARD.md). He fixes
context once from the profile pane and then trades a bare 1-minute chart,
which makes this the one order-flow method in the corpus that needs no live
tape. And he never mentions cost: our [own ES tick work](../UL5QOCSKnU0/CARD.md)
puts a round turn at $17 a contract, 0.17 R of friction on a two-point stop.

Nothing here is a trade. The lesson is drawn in MS Paint over a static
screenshot. That is honest, and it means the video illustrates a method rather
than evidencing one.
