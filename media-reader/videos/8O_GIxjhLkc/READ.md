# Read: Textbook ES Short Using Smart Money & Order Flow

> **A replayed, paper-traded ES short told bar by bar: higher-timeframe imbalances set the bias, a weak retest of 7,800 shows traps and exhaustion, aggressive sellers arrive, and a one-minute break of structure triggers a 5.5-point short for about 1.2R.**

- **Channel:** Trader Dale (presented by "Dan from Funded Trader Academy") · **Uploaded:** 2026-08-19 · **Duration:** 11:44
- **Card:** [CARD.md](CARD.md) · **Watch:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8O_GIxjhLkc

## In brief

Not Dale, and not live. An associate named Dan replays the ES session of
Monday 17 August 2026 on TradingView's Bar Replay with a paper-trading panel,
and the opening slide says every trade shown is hypothetical. What he shows is
the full decision sequence for one short.

The frame is Smart Money Concepts: hourly and four-hour "imbalances" drawn as
boxes, a bearish one injected on Friday sitting under the week's bullish flow.
Into the open he wants price to come down and show him which imbalance wins.
It grinds lower for an hour and a half while he does nothing, until a new
bearish hourly imbalance forms and his "cautiously bullish bias" is shot. Then
a weak bounce into 7,800 prints traps, exhaustion and a burst of aggressive
selling on his bubble indicator, the one-minute structure breaks, and he sells.

Entry 7,795.50, stop 7,800, target 7,790 at the prior swing low. It fills. One
trade, no statistic, and a reward he concedes is "not the greatest".

## The argument

The video's real content is the order in which he lets himself become
confident, so that is worth following.

He begins on the higher timeframe. The prior week consolidated, Thursday broke
to a new all-time high and fell back into the range, and Friday printed a
bearish inside day. That leaves two imbalances in contention on Monday morning:
Friday's bearish one and the broader bullish flow beneath it. His stance into
the open is conditional in both directions. He would like to see price move
down and then watch how it responds: failure inside the bearish zone, a rip
back up, or a displacing move lower. If it displaces up instead, he will trade
that. This is the same shape as Carmine Rosato's context step in
[the CLC episode](../7facFfjQ0UE/CARD.md), a trend and a level where the
auction should react, arrived at through different vocabulary. The Smart Money
Decode X series gives the vocabulary itself: a fair value gap is the void where
no resting limit orders sat, and the standing framework is
[daily structure, four-hour setup, one-hour or fifteen-minute entry](../EW_TtV_rgzY/CARD.md).
Dan runs the same ladder one rung lower, with the one-minute chart as the
trigger.

The session opens and price grinds down into the four-hour imbalance. He is
explicit that there is no clarity yet, and the phrase he uses for what he is
waiting for, a "moment of clarity", recurs until it arrives. Along the way the
bubble overlay gives him the order-flow read: aggressive bears dominating, and
"some bulls step in here, they get absorbed and run over". That is absorption
in the same sense the Vorwald channel defines it in
[its order-book video](../usho6UVLqkE/CARD.md), a participant who keeps
hitting a level while price fails to advance, and it is Carmine's
trapped-participants signal, which his
[volume episode](../w7tvJCuZAq8/CARD.md) turns into a fade. Dan does not fade
it. He reads the absorbed bulls as one more piece of bearish evidence and
keeps waiting. The indicator also stamps BULL TRAP and BEAR TRAP labels on the
chart, which is the premise of
[Carmine's retail-versus-institution episode](../0QlGCz6U_1g/CARD.md) turned
into a plotted signal; how the indicator decides a trap has occurred is never
explained.

The clarity comes an hour and a half in. The initial balance low breaks, a new
bearish hourly imbalance and a fifteen-minute one form, and he states the
plan: if price pops up into that hourly imbalance and shows sell signatures,
he is "more than happy to take a short in here to retrace back down to that
prior swing low". Location and target are fixed before the trade exists. The
retest is the teaching passage. The bounce is weak, a single green bubble on
the way up, "just some passive bulls pushing price up", and at the 7,800
psychological level he sees potential traps, exhaustion, and then one bubble
with a dark ring that his tool reads as more than 50 contracts per second of
aggressive selling. His checklist at that point is explicit: downtrend,
potential trap, exhaustion, sellers pumping in, and what he still needs is a
shift in control and a break of structure.

Here the corpus divides, and Dan sits on one side of it. The SMDX series
[defines a break of structure as a candle close beyond the most recent swing](../YwqfGuktXrc/CARD.md)
and forbids entry before it; Dan enters when he sees "heavy, aggressive
bearish flow closing strong with momentum through this structure break", a
close plus the tape behind it. Carmine's CLC episode argues that waiting for
the close is exactly what widens the stop, and the numbers here illustrate his
point. Selling at 7,795.50 with the stop at 7,800 and the target at 7,790 is
4.5 points of risk for 5.5 points of reward, about 1.2R, and Dan says as much:
"not the greatest RR initially". His answer is to tighten the stop as price
moves, and he did, to 7,798.50, before the target filled and cancelled it.

Two things keep this from being more than a specimen. The headline "6 and 1/2
point" trade is the hand-drawn markup on the intro chart; the replay execution
got 5.5. And it is one hypothetical trade with a proprietary indicator doing
the order-flow reading. The Vorwald channel's
[statistics video](../NkQeOVDTAec/CARD.md) asks for two to three hundred
trades before any figure means anything; this is a sample of one, and Dale's
own method, in [his volume-profile guide](../vVMJa7dyYWE/CARD.md), is a
different thing from what his associate shows here. What the video does
demonstrate is discipline: criteria stated before the entry, the stop placed,
the target at a structural level, and nothing done for ninety minutes.
